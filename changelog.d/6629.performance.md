- **jq: a `foreach` whose EXTRACT is an array of reads of the state is 1.8-3.7x faster** (#6629).
  `[foreach range(n) as $i ({a: 1, b: 2}; .a += 1; [.a, .b])]` now builds the EXTRACT's array on the owned
  tree, as one holding the state itself already did (#6581); before, every step ran it on the evaluator's
  general route. 400,000 steps take 160 ms instead of 368 ms on an Apple M4 Pro and 200 ms instead of 494 ms
  on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X; over a ten-key state 0.27x of the old time on both, and `[.a, .n]` moving an array
  slot out of the state 0.55x on the M4 Pro and 0.47x on the 7950X. Whether an EXTRACT is such a constructor
  is decided once per fold instead of walked for every step, and a fork stops offering a shape the state never
  fits (`[.a, .a]`) after four declines; a shape the fold never takes, or one that declines, is within 0-3% of
  the old time (interleaved, median of 11, with a base-against-base control). An object EXTRACT with no `.`
  member is unchanged: the evaluator already answers it off the tree (#6608), and the step was 1.03x on the
  7950X and 0.93-0.95x on the M4 Pro. Output is identical to jq 1.7.1's.
