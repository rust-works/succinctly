- **jq: a path-free `|=` update no longer materializes the document twice, and `map(.field)` is read off the owned copy** (#6122). `.users |= .` and `.users[0].name |= "x"` took the wildcard bridge, which wrote an owned copy of the whole document out as text and indexed it again before running the update. They now go through the same owned door as `=`, `+=` and `//=`, on the one copy, and answer as before (43 update shapes, variables and definitions among them, pinned against jq 1.7.1 stdout and exit status). On a 100 MB `users` document, min of 11 interleaved runs against the commit before: 1.73 s to 1.50 s on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (0.93x to 1.07x of jq 1.7.1) and 1.09 s to 0.95 s on an Apple M4 Pro (1.90x to 2.18x of jq 1.7.1-apple), -13% on both; `.users[].age |= .+1` and `.users[0].name = "x"` are within ±1% at 100 MB.

  `.users |= map(.age)`, the shape the issue opened with, still took the bridge for its right side: `map(.age)` was not a stage the owned evaluator answered, so the array was written out as text and indexed again to read one field of each element, and the update then ran over the whole document written out as text once more. `map` of a chain of static `.name` / `.[n]` steps over an owned array is now answered from the array itself, and the owned update step takes it as a right side (an object, a scalar, an element a step would raise on, yq mode and a node a binding shares keep the bridge). On the same 100 MB document (74 MB on disk), min of 11 interleaved runs against the commit before: `.users |= map(.age)` goes from 1.58 s to 0.99 s on the 7950X (0.80x to 1.28x of jq 1.7.1) and from 0.82 s to 0.42 s on the M4 Pro (0.92x to 1.79x of jq 1.7.1-apple), and `.users |= (map(.age) | length)` from 0.76x to 1.21x and from 0.90x to 1.77x; `.users |= .` and a base-against-base control read within ±1%.
