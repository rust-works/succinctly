- **jq: a path-free `|=` update no longer materializes the document twice** (#6122). `.users |= .` and `.users[0].name |= "x"` took the wildcard bridge, which wrote an owned copy of the whole document out as text and indexed it again before running the update. They now go through the same owned door as `=`, `+=` and `//=`, on the one copy, and answer as before (43 update shapes, variables and definitions among them, pinned against jq 1.7.1 stdout and exit status). On a 100 MB `users` document, min of 11 interleaved runs against the commit before: 1.73 s to 1.50 s on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (0.93x to 1.07x of jq 1.7.1) and 1.09 s to 0.95 s on an Apple M4 Pro (1.90x to 2.18x of jq 1.7.1-apple), -13% on both; `.users[].age |= .+1` and `.users[0].name = "x"` are within ±1% at 100 MB. `.users |= map(.age)`, the shape the issue opened with, was left at 0.65x (7950X) and 0.71x (M4 Pro) of jq by that change, because its time was the filter's own copy of the array: `map(.age)` is not a stage the owned evaluator answers, so the array was written out as text and indexed again to read one field of each element. `map` of a chain of static `.name` / `.[n]` steps over an owned array is now answered from the array itself (an object, a scalar, an element a step would raise on, yq mode and a node a binding shares keep the bridge). On the same 100 MB document, min of 11 interleaved runs against the commit before: `.users |= map(.age)` goes from 1.57 s to 1.24 s on the 7950X (0.81x to 1.02x of jq 1.7.1) and from 0.87 s to 0.59 s on the M4 Pro (0.92x to 1.36x of jq 1.7.1-apple), and `.users |= map(.name)` from 0.75x to 0.96x and from 1.10x to 1.63x; `.users |= .` is within ±1%, and a base-against-base control reads within 1%. yq mode and an update whose body needs a path context keep the bridge.
