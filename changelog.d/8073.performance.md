- **The release binary orders the regex engine's code with the rest, and its link-order list is regenerated** (#8073).
  `scripts/link-order/link.py` resolved the list only against the object rustc makes, which with fat LTO holds every Rust function; since #7965 the vendored Oniguruma's C objects come in the `libsuccinctly` rlib instead, so none of the 57 Oniguruma functions a regex run executes was ordered, and each kept pages of the binary resident outside the ordered block.
  It now reads the link's archives as well, and the list is regenerated from 345515b56, where 5.3% of the old one's names no longer resolved (82 of 1533): the new one resolves 1460 of its 1462 names on x86_64 Linux (the two others are glibc's `fma` and `floor`).
  On a Ryzen 9 7950X, peak RSS falls by 280 KiB on `users_regex` and 394 KiB on `strings_test` at 1 MB; over 8 seeded layouts, startup is 1.2% faster and `ndjson_identity` 3.0% (1 MB) and 2.0% (10 MB), `wide_map_values` is 0.4% slower, and 16 other rows are flat.
  On an Apple M4 Pro, peak RSS falls by 48-120 KiB on each of the 6 rows measured; times are flat within an A/A' control of up to ±3.7%.
