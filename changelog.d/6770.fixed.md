- **yq: `map(f)` with a union body runs each branch over every member, as in yq** (#6770). yq's `map` runs its body once over the
  whole list of members, so `map(. + 1, . + 2)` over `[1, 2, 3]` is `[2, 3, 4, 3, 4, 5]` (the first branch over every member, then the
  second), `map(.n, .m)` over a list of mappings is every `.n`, then every `.m`, and `map(select(. > 1), 9)` is `[2, 3, 9, 9, 9]`.
  `succinctly yq` ran the body per member and interleaved the branches (`[2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5]`). A union-bodied `map` over a sequence
  or a mapping with members now takes the walk `[.[] | f]` already took, and answers as a node of its own, as `map`'s result is; a scalar, `null` and an empty mapping keep their answers, and
  `succinctly jq` still interleaves, as jq does. Still different: a union-bodied `map` on the right of an assignment (`|=`, `=`, `with`) or on a value the
  program computed first (`[.[]] | map(., 0)`), and bodies that read their own path or key (`map(path, key)`); the plain pipe
  `[.[] | (a, b)]` shares each.
