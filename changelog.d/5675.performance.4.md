- **jq: a string is written by copying its escape-free text whole** (#5675). `write_json_body_jq`, the writer under the re-index bridge, the lazy printer and the stream path, wrote one `char` at a time (an encode and a capacity check each) to escape a string that almost always holds nothing to escape. It now scans bytes for the four things jq escapes (a quote, a backslash, a C0 control and DEL) and copies the text between two hits as one span, as the yq writer already did. Output is byte-identical. Interleaved min of 9 on an Apple M4 Pro against `main` at `b409cb2b3`, 10 MB inputs: `-R tojson` over 200-byte plain lines 122.0 -> 72.3 ms (-41%) and over NDJSON records 137.9 -> 122.6 ms (-11%), `-R '[.,1]|length'` over the plain lines 75.9 -> 57.8 ms and over the records 96.2 -> 89.9 ms, `-R '{a:.}'` 131.1 -> 119.9 ms, `-R .` 47.3 -> 40.5 ms and `tojson` over a 7 MB users document 92.9 -> 85.0 ms; lines of 1-6 characters are flat (671 -> 660 ms), and text that is mostly escapes costs about 2% (358.7 -> 366.2 ms, a string of quotes, backslashes and tabs). The Apple M5 Max reads the same (`-R tojson` over plain lines 116.5 -> 69.1 ms). x86_64 was not measured, and the boxes were shared, so the ratios are the evidence and the absolute times are not quiet-machine times.
