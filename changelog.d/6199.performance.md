- **jq: `path |= f` of a static path into a large JSON document runs `f` on the document instead of on a copy of all of it** (#6199).
  The update copied every node of the document into one owned value before it ran. When the path reaches at least half of a
  document of 65 536 nodes or more (about 1 MB) and `f` starts by reducing the target (`map(..)` of a projection, `length`, `add`,
  `first`, `last`, `limit`, `first(..)` of navigation, an index or a slice), the target is checked by one scan, `f` runs on it in the
  document, and the containers on the path are rebuilt around its output, so what `f` does not keep is never built. On a 100 MB
  `users` document, `.users |= length` takes 134 ms instead of 390 ms on an Apple M4 Pro and 185 ms instead of 864 ms on an AMD
  Ryzen 9 7950X, `|= first` 122 ms instead of 649 ms (169 instead of 1301), `|= map(.age)` 377 ms instead of 857 (453 instead of
  1555), `|= map(.age + 1)` 480 ms instead of 914 (562 instead of 1614), `|= (map(.age) | add)` 411 ms instead of 890 (523 instead of 1626),
  and `|= .[:10]` 629 ms instead of 706 (1267 instead of 1457); the same updates are 42-82% faster at 1 MB and 10 MB. Peak memory
  on the M4 Pro falls from 624 MB to 92 MB for `|= length`, from 798 MB to 92 MB for `|= first` and from 830 MB to 152 MB for
  `|= map(.age)`. The answers are the ones the copy gave: a filter that answers nothing or raises, a `?//` retry, a `map` under
  a consumer that stops early, a document that is malformed anywhere, a repeated key on the path and a path that does not land
  all take the route they took before, and a filter that keeps most of the target (`.`, `sort_by`, `reverse`, `map(select(..))`) is
  not taken (within 1.5% either way). An update inside a per-record loop whose filter is one of these reducers
  (`[.users[] | .name |= length]`) pays for the look, about 1% and at most 2% at 10 MB and 100 MB on both machines.
