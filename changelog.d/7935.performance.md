- **jq: a comma with a `.[]` branch over a collected array no longer writes the array out and indexes it** (#7935).
  `.[], .`, `., .[]`, `.[]?, .` and `.[], .[]` over an array or object the program built (`[...] | .[], .`) declined
  the owned door and went through the re-index bridge, which writes the value out as JSON and indexes it again. A
  recursion that collects each level and yields `.[], .` of it (`def f: if . > 0 then [. - 1 | f] | .[], . else .
  end;`) wrote out a value whose text doubles per level, so `first(50 | f)` reached 2.6 GB in 5 s with no answer,
  where jq 1.7.1 answers `0` at once. The children of such a branch are now handed on as they are read, each still
  sharing its storage, so the sink sees the first before the rest are copied. Indicative, Apple silicon laptop, peak
  RSS and time, output identical:

  | Program                                                       | jq 1.7.1        | Before          | After           |
  |---------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
  | `first(20 \| f)`                                              | 2.6 MB, 0.00 s  | 26.5 MB, 0.07 s | 5.0 MB, 0.01 s  |
  | `first(24 \| f)`                                              | 2.6 MB, 0.00 s  | 359 MB, 0.73 s  | 5.0 MB, 0.01 s  |
  | `first(25 \| f)`                                              | 2.6 MB, 0.00 s  | 689 MB, 1.38 s  | 5.1 MB, 0.00 s  |
  | `first(50 \| f)`                                              | 2.6 MB, 0.00 s  | killed at 5 s   | 5.5 MB, 0.01 s  |
  | `first(22 \| g)`, `g` yielding `.[], .[]`                     | 150 MB, 0.26 s  | 230 MB, 0.49 s  | 130 MB, 0.07 s  |
  | `[22 \| g] \| length`                                         | 309 MB, 0.44 s  | 539 MB, 0.74 s  | 382 MB, 0.12 s  |

  A comma no door answers (`(.[] | .), .`, `(range(length) as $i | .[$i]), .`) still takes the bridge.
