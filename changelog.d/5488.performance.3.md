- **jq: a body that reads its variable late, behind stages that are not arithmetic, is linear in its stage count** (#5488, third step).
  `. as $x | (<n - 1 stages of [.][0], floor or fabs> | . + $x)` copied nothing more than once per value after the second step, but each of the `n` tails of its pipe asked whether it holds a marker, which re-walked the stages to the last one, and each re-entry walked them again for the marker a rewrite would change: 43% of a profile at 80 stages, `n` walks of up to `n` stages per value.
  What is true of a shared storage (the position of the last marker, of the last node that can start a resolver invocation, and the markers themselves) is now worked out once and every view of it answers from that, found from the end of the storage, so a body whose last stage reads the variable answers at once.
  A pipe that holds the node in its first 8 stages pays what it did.
  At n = 10, 20, 40, 80 on an Apple M4 Pro, `[.][0]` stages read last take 0.088, 0.194, 0.454, 1.141 s and now 0.090, 0.190, 0.377, 0.778 s (2.0x per doubling, was 2.6x), `floor` 1.080 s and now 0.526 s at 80, `fabs` 1.231 s and 0.708 s, and a read in the middle of the pipe 0.805 s and 0.734 s; on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X `[.][0]` 1.763 s and 1.149 s at 80, `floor` 1.251 s and 0.755 s, `fabs` 1.519 s and 1.026 s, mid-pipe 1.171 s and 1.045 s (interleaved min of 5, output identical to jq 1.7.1).
  The read-first and `. + 1`-then-read controls, and ten shapes that do not read late (reduce, foreach, `.[] |=`, destructuring, `def f($a)`, a plain pipe, a late `def`) and eight queries over a 7 MB document, are within 0.99x-1.02x on both; the every-stage shape is within 0.99x-1.01x on the M4 Pro and 1.01x-1.03x on the 7950X, whose retired instructions are +0.5% (`[.][0]` read last: -14.3%).
