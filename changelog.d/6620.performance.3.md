- **jq: a `--seq` stream with a diagnostic is walked once** (#6620).
  The clean check that sends a `--seq` run down the lazy route walks the whole stream with jq's reader, and when a record is truncated it threw that walk away and the route that reads such a stream walked the same bytes again (and read a named file a second time). The route now takes the check's walk, with the faults it queues, for a stream read from stdin or one file, and places the values' locations from it without a second pass over the reader; a slurp, several files and a file that did not open keep the walk they made. `--seq -r` over a 100 MB stream whose last record is truncated, before and after, interleaved, median of 9: `.name` 1.50 s to 1.07 s (-28%), `{id,name}` 1.94 s to 1.50 s (-23%) and `select(.age>30)|.name` 1.68 s to 1.24 s (-26%) on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, `.name` 1.31 s to 0.98 s (-25%), `{id,name}` 1.70 s to 1.36 s (-20%) and `select(.age>30)|.name` 1.41 s to 1.07 s (-24%) on an Apple M4 Pro (1 MB and 10 MB: -15% to -29%). The programs the owned evaluator declines gain too, `[.id,.name]` and `keys` -14% on the 7950X and -12% to -14% on the M4 Pro, and a program that reads `input` does not change (within -1.6% to +2.3%). Against jq 1.7.1 that is `.name` at 0.98x (from 0.69x) and `{id,name}` at 1.12x (from 0.86x) on the 7950X, and 1.20x (from 0.90x) and 1.56x (from 1.26x) on the M4 Pro. Output is identical on all 36 configurations of each A/B and to jq 1.7.1's on a program-by-stream differential over stdin and over a named file, and peak memory is unchanged (158 MB to 150 MB on the 7950X, 159 MB on the M4 Pro; jq: 3 MB).
