- **jq: `length` of a number literal keeps its digits, as in jq 1.8.2** (#7534). `length` of a number took the absolute value of its double, as jq 1.7.1 does, so `-100000000000000000001 | length` was `1e+20`, `1E-400 | length` was `0` and `1.50 | length` was `1.5`. jq 1.8 fixed that (jqlang/jq#3275), and `succinctly jq` now follows it: the result is the literal's absolute value with every digit (`100000000000000000001`, `1E-400`, `1.50`), and a negative zero is `0` (`-0.0 | length` is `0.0`). It holds for input documents, `-s`, `inputs`, `--argjson`, `--slurpfile` and program literals. A computed number, NaN, the infinities and non-numbers are unchanged, and so is `succinctly yq`. The difference from the pinned 1.7.1 is recorded in `docs/compliance/jq/limitations.md`.
