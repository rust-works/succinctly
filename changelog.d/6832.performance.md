- **A bound string of 4 KiB or more is held behind a refcount in its marker, so a loop that reads it no longer copies it every round** (#6832). A string is stored inline, and the marker that stands for a bound variable was cloned once per pipe step (`rewrite_markers`, at every re-entry of the evaluator), so `"abcd" * 1750000 | . as $t | [range(100) | . as $w | ($t | length)]` copied the 7 MB string 100 times (`memmove` was the whole profile). Those clones are now pointer bumps: in `succinctly yq --jq-extensions` that loop takes 25 ms instead of 565 ms on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (22x; 1 MB 12.8x, 10 MB 23x, and 7 MB over 400 rounds 84x, the cost no longer growing with the rounds) and 12.4 ms instead of 29.0 ms on an Apple M4 Pro (2.3x; 400 rounds 5.7x), where macOS's allocator hid most of the copy; with a stage that works on the string every round (`ascii_upcase | length`) it is 1.6x on the 7950X, and in `succinctly jq` the same loop is 1.8x there and level on the M4 Pro. A string below 4 KiB stays inline (3 KB over 20,000 rounds: 0.99x and 1.01x), and so does one nothing binds (a string-building `reduce`: 0.98x and 1.04x). The output is unchanged.
