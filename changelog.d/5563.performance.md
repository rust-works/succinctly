- **jq: a global `l` regex search is linear in the matches, not quadratic** (#5563). An `l` search returns the longest match anywhere at or after its start, so it reads the rest of the text, and `match`, `scan`, `sub`, `gsub` and `splits` with `g` ran one per match. One forward pass over the starts now serves every search of the same text (the longest match among the starts from there on, the earliest of equals), and a pattern with `\G`, which ties a match to where its search began, keeps the search per match. `[match("a|ab+";"gl")] | length` on `"ab "` repeated 5,000, 10,000 and 20,000 times (release builds, interleaved, min of 3, output identical to jq's) went from 0.97 s, 3.79 s and 15.3 s to 7 ms, 9 ms and 14 ms on an Apple M5 Max; jq 1.7.1 takes 0.21 s, 0.93 s and 3.5 s. The pass keeps a 24-byte entry per start that matches, for the length of one call.
