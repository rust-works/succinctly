- **jq: the compact-echo gate checks a wide object's keys with one exactly sized table** (#3333). Past 16 keys `scan_canonical_object` grew a `KeyHashes` table from empty, so on a wide object every key was probed once and rehashed about once more (83 instructions per key, 6.9% of `wide` 2 MB `-c .`). It now collects the key hashes and settles them at the closing `}` in a table sized for exactly that many keys. Interleaved wall-clock, `-c .`: `wide` 2 MB (136k keys) −25% on a 7950X and −22% on an M4 Pro, 560k and 700k keys −25% to −31%, arrays of 1,000-key objects −17% and of 100-key objects −9% to −10% on both, 17-24-key objects −5% to +1%; 7950X instructions −6.7% on `wide`. Objects of 16 keys or fewer are unchanged in instructions (`users` and `data` read within the layout band against a holdout). Output is identical on every row and the same objects are declined (the 786,432-key ceiling is unchanged); a repeated key inside a wide object is now found at the end of the object rather than at the key, so a repeat near the front of a 136k- or 700k-key object (which still ends in the full re-render) costs 2% to 4% more wall-clock and 5% more instructions. A sort of the collected hashes was measured and rejected: a further −7% to −10% on the 7950X at 136k keys and above, but +5% to +10% worse on the M4 Pro at every width above 17 keys and +5% to +7% worse on the 7950X at 100 and 1,000 keys.
