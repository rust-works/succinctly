- **jq: a loop that reads an array the program built by index or by a narrow slice is no longer quadratic** (#6666).
  `[range(N)] | [range(0; length; 2) as $i | .[$i:$i+2]]`, `.users | map(.id) | [range(0; length; 2) as $i | .[$i]]` and
  their `.[3:5]` and `limit`/`first` forms walked the whole array on every read: the reindex bridge's document had no
  element index, and the slice arm collected and decoded every element before taking its range. The bridge's document now
  gets the same per-document memos as a document read, and the slice reads only its range. On an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X
  (jq 1.7.1 beside, same output): `.[$i]` over 20,000 elements 3,105 ms to 8 ms (jq 9 ms), `.[$i:$i+2]` 13,524 ms to 14 ms
  (jq 12 ms) and over 200,000 elements 104 ms (jq 107 ms); over a 6.9 MB `users` document
  `.users | map(.id) | [range(0; length; 2) as $i | .[$i:$i+2]]` went from an allocation abort after 43 s to 85 ms (jq
  150 ms). Reads that were already fast are unchanged (`.users[4242]`, `.[100:200]`, `del(.[0:100])` within 1-2%). The
  front slice of an owned array in a loop that keeps or recurses over its tails is a separate cost (#6659). The M4 Pro was not
  measured.
