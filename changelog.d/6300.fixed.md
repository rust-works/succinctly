- **jq: a loop body whose consuming way holds an assertion ends at the body's empty way, as Oniguruma's loop does** (#6300). `"ba" | [match("(?:b*|\\Ba)+") | .string]` printed `["ba",""]` and `"ba" | [match("(?:b*|a\\z)+") | .string]` printed `["ba",""]`; jq 1.7.1 answers `["b","",""]` for both. An assertion beside an item that consumes text (`\Ba`, `a\z`, `^a`, `a$`) only decides whether that way matches, so it is copied with the other ways and no longer keeps the loop as written; a way that matches nothing only where an assertion holds (`(?:b*|\b)+`) still does. Over 20,000 generated loops with one way that can match nothing and one or two ways holding an assertion, on 13 strings under `g`, the 362 that differed from jq now agree and none changed the other way (`docs/compliance/jq/limitations.md`).
