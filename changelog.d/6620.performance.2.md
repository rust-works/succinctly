- **jq: a `--seq` stream with a diagnostic reads each record where it is for the programs that can** (#6620).
  The records of such a stream reach the main loop as owned values, which it wrote back out as text, indexed and read through a cursor before the program ran. A record is now handed to the owned evaluator's own doors, as a `--stream` event is, and a program none of them answers (`[.id,.name]`, `keys`, one that reads `input`) reads the record's index as before; a run of four such records closes the door for 64 records, and it asks again after that, so a stream whose records change shape gets it back. `--seq -r` over a 100 MB stream whose last record is truncated, before and after, interleaved, median of 9: `.name` 2.27 s to 1.50 s (-34%) and `{id,name}` 2.95 s to 1.94 s (-34%) on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, `.name` 2.06 s to 1.37 s (-34%) and `{id,name}` 2.65 s to 1.80 s (-32%) on an Apple M4 Pro (a truncated first record, and 1 MB and 10 MB streams: -29% to -36%). That is 0.70x of jq 1.7.1 on `.name` and 0.85x on `{id,name}` on the 7950X (from 0.46x and 0.56x), and 0.89x and 1.24x on the M4 Pro (from 0.59x and 0.85x). The programs the door declines are not faster: on the M4 Pro they read -4.4% to +1.7% (control -1.7% to +1.6%), and on the 7950X +1% to +3% with one 1 MB row at +5% (control -1.1% to +1.3%; a build with the door forced off reads +0.9% at the median against the same baseline, so about half of that is code layout). Output is identical to the old route's on every configuration and to jq 1.7.1's on a 10,000-case program-by-stream differential of the final code (`scripts/jq-seq-door-sweep.py`), and peak memory is unchanged (159 MB on the M4 Pro, 157 MB on the 7950X, against jq's 3 MB). The walks that run twice over such a stream stay with #6620.
