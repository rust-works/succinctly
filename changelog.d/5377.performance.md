- **jq: `last(f)` holds one value at a time** (#5377). It collected every output of `f` before taking the final one, so `last(range(30000000) * 2)` was killed at 3 GB where jq stays at 2.5 MB (since #5292 lifted the `range` cap there is no limit to bound it). Unless `f` is navigation alone (`last(.[])`, `last(.a[] | select(.b))`, `last(..)`), `last(f)` now runs as the `reduce f as $x (null; $x)` jq defines it by, which streams its source: an empty `f` still answers `null`, and an error after the final value (`last(1, error("x"))`), a `break` or a `halt` is `reduce`'s own. A navigation keeps the collecting route, whose outputs are cursors into the document and not values to decode. Measured on an Apple M5 Max (release builds, min of one run): peak RSS of `last(range(3000000) * 2)` 399 MB → 102 MB (the 97 MB floor is the 70 MB document loaded beside it), `last(range(0;20000000;3))` 304 MB → 102 MB and `last(.users[] | {id})` over that document 355 MB → 97 MB; with no document, 312 MB → 15 MB and 217 MB → 14 MB. The price is `reduce`'s per-step cost: those take 0.19 s → 0.39 s, 0.14 s → 0.59 s and 0.37 s → 0.76 s, against jq 1.7.1's 0.54 s, 3.34 s and (for the first) more.
