- **jq: `last(f)` over a generator holds one value at a time** (#5377). It collected every output of `f` before taking the final one, so `last(range(30000000) * 2)` was killed at 3 GB where jq stays at 2.5 MB (since #5292 lifted the `range` cap there is no limit to bound it). When `f` has a generator in it (`range`, `repeat`, `while`, `until`, `recurse(f)`) `last(f)` now runs as the `reduce f as $x (null; $x)` jq defines it by, which streams its source: an empty `f` still answers `null`, and an error after the final value (`last(1, error("x"))`), a `break` or a `halt` is `reduce`'s own. A navigation (`last(.[])`, `last(.a[] | select(.b))`) keeps the collecting route, whose outputs are cursors into the document and not values to decode. Measured on an Apple M5 Max (release builds): peak RSS of `last(range(3000000) * 2)` 312 MB → 15 MB, `last(range(0;20000000;3))` 217 MB → 14 MB, `last(range(3000000) | select(. > 5))` 245 MB → 17 MB, and `last(0 | while(. < 3000000; . + 1))` unchanged at 103 MB (the loop's own state). The price is `reduce`'s per-step cost: those take 1.01 s → 2.62 s, 0.07 s → 0.51 s and 0.86 s → 0.45 s, against jq 1.7.1's 0.54 s, 3.34 s and 0.97 s.
