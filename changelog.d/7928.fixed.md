- **`succinctly jq` no longer runs a recursion to all of its outputs first when a closure argument wraps `try ... catch` around a body that yields and then raises** (#7928). `def f(g; $n): if $n > 0 then f(try (g + (1, error("x"))) catch 0; $n - 1) + f(g; $n - 1) else g end; first(f(null; 4) | select(. > 30))` is `32` in jq 1.7.1 and 1.8.2, at once; since #7787 `succinctly jq` built all 14,929,920 outputs of `f(null; 4)` before `first` saw one (about 354 billion instructions on Apple silicon), and did not finish at depth 5, whose output count is about 3.7e16. #7787 counted a `,` with an error after its output as answering once, and a `try` whose body and handler each answer once as answering once, but jq's `try` catches that later error (or a `break`) and runs its handler too: `try (1, error("x")) catch 0` is `1, 0`. A `try` with a handler that yields is now run first only over a body that cannot raise after its output. The program now retires about 26 million instructions at depth 4 and 34 million at depth 6, process start included. A read of a bare parameter under such a `try` counts as a body that cannot raise late when no argument of the call, or of the recursive calls in its body, can: `def f(g; $n): if $n > 0 then f(try g catch 0; $n - 1) + f(g; $n - 1) else g end; [f(1; 16)] | length` is `1`, as in jq. A tree recursion whose argument does raise late (`f(try (g, error("x")) catch 0; $n - 1) + f(g; $n - 1)`) streams instead, and from depth 12 is refused with `exceeded maximum recursion depth` where jq answers `first(f(1; 12))`; the jq limitations page records it.
