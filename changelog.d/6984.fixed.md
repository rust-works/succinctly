- **jq: a slice bound below zero with a fractional part is added to the length before it is rounded, as jq does** (#6984). `jq`'s `parse_slice` adds the length to a negative bound in double arithmetic and rounds the sum, so `-1e-66 + 5` is exactly `5.0` and `[1,2,3,4,5] | .[-1e-66:]` is `[]`; `succinctly jq` floored the bound first and answered `[5]`. The same sum decides `-1.0000000000000002` (`[5]`, not `[4,5]`), an end of `-0.9999999999999999` (`[1,2,3,4]`, not the whole array: #3404's past-the-end answer was only right for ends that round to zero at every length), and a bound over a long array (`[range(1;1000001)] | .[-1e-10:]` keeps one element where `-5e-11` keeps none). Strings, `.[{"start":s,"end":e}]` and the read routes of both evaluators now agree with jq 1.7.1 on a sweep of 24 bounds against lengths 0-13 and a million; the path, write and `del` routes already did.
