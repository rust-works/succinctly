- **jq: a plain filter over one regular file reads it a window at a time, so its peak memory no longer grows with the file** (#6920). `jq -c .`, `jq -r .name` and `jq 'select(...)'` (no `-n`, no `input`) over a regular file of 4 MiB or more used to read it whole and split every document into a span first: 127 to 129 MiB at 100 MiB of NDJSON on x86_64 and 122 to 123 on aarch64, where jq's stays at a few MB. They now hold a 1 MiB window and the document being run, 10 to 12 MiB on x86_64 and 13 on aarch64 (that machine's floor for any process), flat from 10 MiB to 100 MiB. Output, error wording, exit status and the `(at file:N)` lines are the whole-file route's, checked against it on faults at several positions, an unterminated last document, a BOM, invalid UTF-8, pretty-printed and one-line streams, a document larger than a window, a named pipe, `/dev/stdin` and depth refusals. Time at 100 MiB on a 7950X: `.` −3.5%, `.name` −3.9%, `select` −5.4%, `keys` −5.1%; neutral on an M4 Pro; a file that is one document (single-line or pretty-printed) reads as before, within 2%. Standard input, a pipe, several files, programs that call `input`/`inputs`, `-R`, `--seq`, `-s` and `--validate` still read the input whole.
