- **jq: a `--seq` file with one bad record is read a window at a time up to that record** (#7262).
  One regular file of 4 MiB or more (named, or redirected onto standard input) that holds a record
  other than `RS value LF` was read whole wherever that record stood. It is now read as a `--seq`
  pipe is (#7164): windowed up to its first record that is not plain, and only the rest is held,
  once. `--seq -r .name` over 100 MiB whose last record is cut short (`seq_field_bad_last`) went
  from 1066.5 to 766.4 ms (-28.1%) and 146.1 to 10.7 MiB peak RSS on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, and
  from 1004.1 to 695.3 ms (-30.8%) and 152.2 to 7.4 MiB on an Apple M4 Pro; a bad record half way
  through holds 79-80 MiB where the whole read held 146-152. The hand-off no longer copies the rest
  of the stream either, which also helps a `--seq` pipe that turns early: one whose bad record is
  1% in went from 208.6 to 145.1 MiB (7950X) and 255.4 to 156.3 MiB (M4 Pro). Output is identical.
  A bad first record still costs the whole file, and so does a bad record in any of several files.
