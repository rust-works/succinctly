- **jq: a string's `length` and `startswith`/`endswith` are answered from the string in more composed positions** (#5712, #5691 follow-up).
  #5710 answered them only as a lone last stage. A leading `length` or `startswith`/`endswith` that a later stage runs on (`$s|length|tostring`, also inside a parenthesised pipe) and a `select` or `until`/`while` condition (`select(length>3)`) now take the same route instead of writing the string out and indexing it again once per crossing. On a 6.6 MB `--rawfile` on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X: `$s|length|tostring` 33 ms to 13 ms, `$s|select(length>3)|length` 44 ms to 13 ms, `$s|startswith("a")|not` 32 ms to 12 ms.
  A loop condition was read through the bridge once per round (`"" | until(length >= N; . + "x") | length`: N+2 reindexes, 271 ms at N=20,000 and 1,054 ms at N=40,000); it is now one crossing, 8 ms and 25 ms. The loop is still quadratic in jq mode, because each round's `length` counts codepoints in an O(n) pass (330 ms at N=160,000); in yq mode `length` is a byte count and the loop is linear.
  A condition over a value nested deeper than 384 levels, such as `select(tostring|length>5)`, is now answered as jq answers it instead of refused with `nesting depth exceeds limit of 384`. Output is otherwise identical in both modes.
  Measured on one machine (x86_64); ARM64 was not. Still through the bridge: a comparison of the length outside a condition (`$s|length>5`), `try length`, a `length` inside a per-element `range` body, a `reduce` or a `def`, and a `select` or `if` over a string inside `[...]`, `map`, `//` or an object value.
