- **jq: `.[$i:][0]` and `.[$i:] | length` no longer build the whole slice to read one element or a count** (#4195). A computed slice converted every element in its range into an owned array, so reading one element (or the length) through it cost O(tail) and a loop over it was quadratic. `E[a:b][k]` (an integer-literal `k`, negative counting from the slice's end) and `E[a:b] | length` now resolve the slice's range and read it directly, converting only the element named. Reading each of 3,000 elements from the back (`[range(length-1;-1;-1) as $i | .[$i:][0]]`): `| length` 3.10 s to 3 ms and `[0]` 2.39 s to 5 ms on a Ryzen 9 7950X, 1.70 s to 4 ms and 1.71 s to 6 ms on an Apple M4 Pro; at 16,000 elements 97.7 s to 9 ms and 76.2 s to 20 ms (7950X), 48.5 s to 9 ms and 49.0 s to 19 ms (M4 Pro). Output is unchanged in `jq` and `yq` mode; the one observable difference is that the other elements of the range are no longer converted, so a malformed element the read does not name no longer raises, as with `.[k]` and `length` (jq rejects such a document at parse). A slice with no such read (`.[$i:$i+8] | 0`, still O(n) per slice) and the position form (`.[$i:][0] | key`) are unchanged.
