- **jq: `split` of a string takes its pieces straight off the text** (#5682). `split(",")` of a value read from a document or a `-R` line materialized the string, wrote it out as JSON, indexed that and had `split` read it back; the writing cost as much as the split. A literal string separator now splits the value's own text; a non-string, a computed separator, the two-argument form and a string that does not decode keep the old route and its wording. `-R 'split(",")|.[2]'` over generated lines, interleaved A/B, min of 11, output identical: 1 MB / 3 MB / 10 MB are -34.8% / -35.8% / -36.0% on a 7950X and -32.5% / -35.5% / -36.2% on an M4 Pro, which takes jq's lead (0.7x of jq before) to parity; `split(",")|.[2]|tonumber` is -25% to -29% on both, and `-R length` is within the harness's noise.
