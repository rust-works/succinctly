- **jq: `del(.. | f)` and `delpaths([path(f)])` hold less memory** (#7789).
  A stage after a descent (`.. | .pad?`, `.. | strings`, `.. | select(. == null)`) rebuilt each result's whole path
  chain rather than sharing the chain of the node it extends, so the paths held memory proportional to their total
  length, quadratic in the depth of a deeply nested document, where they now share it: linear in the nodes the paths
  name. The delete trie keeps a leaf in a few words and a node's few edges in a short list, releases each path once it
  is inserted, and a jq-mode `del` drops its resolved branches before the trie grows. `delpaths([path(f)])` builds its
  path list as `[path(f)] as $p | delpaths($p)` does rather than holding it three times over. Output is unchanged, and
  a stage whose results cannot share a chain (`del(.users[] | select(.score < 100))`) and yq mode build their paths as
  before. Peak RSS on an 18 MB array of 201,000 small records, on the AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (Apple M4 Pro): `del(.. |
  .pad?)` 1724 -> 870 MiB (1718 -> 1141), `del(..|strings)` 1061 -> 748 MiB (1121 -> 849), `del(.. | select(. ==
  null))` 918 -> 744 MiB (991 -> 832) and `delpaths([path(.. | .pad?)])` 1155 -> 695 MiB (1265 -> 641), where jq 1.7.1
  takes 570 / 485 / 450 / 570 MiB (611 / 534 / 493 / 611). On 300 chains of 120 nested objects, `del(.. | .n?)` falls
  from 490 to 43 MiB (426 to 58), below jq's 74 (85).
