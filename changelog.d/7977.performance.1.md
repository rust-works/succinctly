- **jq: the experimental call machine runs a zero-argument tail loop in half the shipped route's instructions** (#7977, step 4 of its migration plan).
  Under `SUCCINCTLY_CALL_MACHINE=1`, a self tail call of a zero-argument `def` that makes no closure over its frame is now a jump back to the function's entry in the frame it runs in, and a binary operator reads a constant right operand where it is kept instead of copying its input for it.
  `def f: if . < 1000000 then . + 1 | f else . end; 0 | f` retires 1,422M instructions, against 3,155M before and 2,875M with the switch off (Apple M5 Max).
  The jump keeps every bound of the call it replaces: the loop cap refuses at the same round with the same wording, and the output bound and byte budget are unchanged.
  A `while` or `until` whose update leaves a finished `try` or label behind (`try (. + 1) catch 0`, `label $x | . + 1`, `first(. + 1, 0)`) now runs in constant memory on the machine, as the shipped routes do, where it was refused at its byte budget after 1-2 million rounds: such fork points are dead, and are dropped at the next round.
  With the switch unset or `0` nothing changes.
