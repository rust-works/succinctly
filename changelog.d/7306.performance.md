- **jq: `min_by(f)`, `max_by(f)` and `add` keep only what they need as they walk an array** (#7306). `min_by`/`max_by` collected every element's cursor and then every element's `[f]` key before comparing any, and `add` collected every cursor before its fold only to check every gap first; all three now take one walk that checks each gap inline and keeps the winner and its key (or the running sum), with jq 1.7.1's tie rules (first of equal minima, last of equal maxima, the NaN order of #6008) and error order unchanged. The full evaluator's `min_by`/`max_by`, which a computed array (`[.[]]`, `map(.)`, a slice) reaches, keeps only the winner too. Over the `numbers` pattern, interleaved, output identical to jq's: `.numbers|max_by(.)` on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X 2006 -> 1044 ms and 1987 -> 130 MB peak RSS at 100 MB (jq 1.7.1: 3200 ms, 985 MB); Apple M4 Pro 1175 -> 971 ms and 1810 -> 126 MB (jq 2316 ms, 1142 MB). `.numbers|add` at 100 MB: 7950X 706 -> 556 ms, 346 -> 128 MB; M4 Pro 630 -> 563 ms, 350 -> 125 MB. `.users|max_by(.score)` at 100 MB: 284 -> 99 MB (7950X), 264 -> 94 MB (M4 Pro). Over a computed array, `[.numbers[]]|max_by(.)` at 100 MB: 7950X 2980 -> 2389 ms and 1939 -> 784 MB (jq 3855 ms, 985 MB); M4 Pro 1870 -> 806 MB (jq 1142 MB). Bare `min`/`max`, `sort`, `sort_by`, `group_by` and `unique_by` are unchanged.
