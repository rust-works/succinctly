- **jq: an object that repeats a key no longer holds every field and every key to collapse it** (#7311).
  jq's rule (a repeated key keeps its first position and takes its last value) was applied by
  materializing the object's fields and owning each key in a map. Only the keys whose hash another key
  shares are decoded now, and the other fields stream as before. On a 100 MB object of 7.1 M keys with
  one repeated key, `.` takes 1.36 s instead of 3.90 s and peaks at 261 MB instead of 2,648 MB on an AMD
  Ryzen 9 7950X (1.08 s instead of 1.76 s, and 311 MB instead of 2,852 MB, on an Apple M4 Pro), which is
  what the same object costs without the repeat when its text cannot be echoed. `-c .` and `-C .` gain
  the same, `map(.) | length` takes 1.60 s instead of 4.06 s (351 MB instead of 2,649 MB), `.[]` 1.77 s
  instead of 3.19 s and `select(true)` 1.64 s instead of 2.64 s (7950X). The saving grows with the
  object: `.` is 53%, 61% and 65% faster at 1, 10 and 100 MB on the 7950X. Objects that repeat no key
  time as before.
