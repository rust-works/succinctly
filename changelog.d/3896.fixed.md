- **jq: a `reduce`/`foreach` container loop variable is one value read twice, as in jq** (#3896).
  `reduce ([nan]) as $a (0; $a == $a)` is `true` in jq and was `false` here (`$a != $a` the reverse), because each `$a` in UPDATE or EXTRACT was
  spliced as a freshly rebuilt literal, so jq's storage-identity shortcut in `==` never saw one handle. The same split answered
  `[$a] == [$a]`, `[$a, $a] | .[0] == .[1]`, `[$a] | IN([$a])`, `[$a] | index([$a])` (`null`, jq `0`), `[$a, $a] - [$a]` and `[$a, $a] | unique | length` (`2`, jq `1`),
  in `foreach` UPDATE and EXTRACT, through destructuring and `?//`, on every INIT fork, and against an outer handle (`[nan] as $n | reduce ($n, $n)
  as $a (null; . == $a)`). A fold whose UPDATE or EXTRACT reads a loop variable *and* can compare it (a `==`/`!=`, an array subtraction, a builtin or a call) now splices
  the element's own storage, as an `as` bind does (#3069); an assignment's path and right-hand side keep their in-place step unless they hold a
  `==`/`!=`, so `reduce .[] as $r ({}; .[$r.name] = $r.score)` still copies nothing (#3138). `succinctly yq` is unchanged. Only NaN-bearing containers can
  tell the two apart; the splice also skips the per-read rebuild, so a comparing fold over 15,000 record elements (`reduce .[] as $r (0; if $r == $r then . + 1 else . end)`, 1.2 MB, Apple M5 Max, interleaved min of 11) went 142 ms to 88 ms (-38%, 1,726M to 737M instructions retired), while the assignment and scalar folds that do not match the gate moved by at most 0.3% in instructions. Pinned by `test_fold_container_loop_variable_keeps_identity_3896` and `fold_loop_variable_identity_gate_3896`.
