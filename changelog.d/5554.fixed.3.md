- **jq: a value built past 384 levels is bounded by the native stack, not refused at 384** (#5554). `reduce range(500) as $i (0; [.]) | length`, `reduce range(400) as $i (0; [.]) | flatten`, `last(foreach range(500) as $i (0; [.])) | length` and, over `def f(n): if n == 0 then 0 else [f(n-1)] end;`, `f(400) | [., 1] | length`, `f(400) | sort | length`, `[f(400)] | map(tojson)`, `f(400) | error` and about 80 other consumers raised `nesting depth exceeds limit of 384` (exit 5) where jq 1.7.1 answers. Most of them refused in the re-index bridge, before the stage itself ran. In `succinctly jq`, every walk over a built value that is past 384 levels now checks ADR-0025's native-stack floor once per level instead, so it answers as jq does (byte-identical with jq on the 27 programs probed at 400 levels, comparing stdout and stderr together, and exit status). Past the floor, about 700,000 levels for the bridge on the 256 MiB release thread on Apple Silicon, it refuses with `nesting depth exceeds limit of the native stack` at exit 5, uncatchable, where jq segfaults. In a sweep of 75 consumers at 600,000 and 3,000,000 levels, every one answered or refused at exit 5. The exceptions were four path builtins at 600,000 levels: their output grows with the square of the depth, and they hit a 6 GB memory cap. A fold whose step has no owned door (`[.]`, `{a: .}`) re-indexes its accumulator every step, so past 384 levels it is now quadratic instead of refused. `succinctly yq` keeps 384.
