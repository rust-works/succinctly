- **jq: `combinations` and `combinations(n)` hand each row on as it is built, as jq does** (#7726).
  Both built the whole product before the first row, so a consumer that stops early still paid for all of it: `[1,2] | first(combinations(22))` took 4.7 GB and 2 to 6 s on an Apple laptop (now 3.9 MB and under 0.01 s; laptop, indicative), and `[limit(3; combinations(1000))]` was refused with `Cannot allocate 2^1000 elements for combinations` where jq answers at once.
  `first`, `limit`, `nth`, `label`/`break`, `isempty`, `any`/`all`, `reduce` and `foreach` now read only the rows they take (`reduce` over 2^20 rows: 1.1 GB to 4 MB), on both the document and the `-n` routes; collecting the whole product (`[combinations(18)] | length`) retires the same instructions as before, within 0.1%.
  A product collected whole that cannot be held is still refused, and so is `combinations(n)` with `n` past 2^29 (`{"a":[1]} | first(combinations(4294967297))` grew to 3 GB before), which neither jq can answer.
