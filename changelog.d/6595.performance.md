- **jq: `recurse(.children[]?)` reads each node's children off the tree instead of re-indexing the node** (#6595).
  A static path ending in `.[]?` (`.children[]?`, `.[]?`, `.a.b[]?`) used to write every visited node, and so
  its whole subtree, out as JSON text and index it again, once per ancestor, so the walk grew with depth at a
  fixed node count. Over 1 million nodes in chains 10, 30 and 60 deep, `[.[] | recurse(.children[]?)] | length`
  went from 3.56, 4.98 and 8.01 s to 1.65, 1.44 and 2.04 s (jq 1.7.1: 1.2-1.3 s), and `| .name` from 2.79, 4.38
  and 7.76 s to 1.00, 0.94 and 1.50 s, which is 1.3-1.4x faster than jq on the shallower chains (AMD Ryzen 9
  7950X, interleaved, median of 5). A step that would raise (`1 | .children[]?` raises in jq: its `?` guards only
  the iteration) still takes the old route, so every error and every output is unchanged. A `recurse` whose
  later stage builds a value (`{id, name}`) is still slower than jq and still grows with depth.
