- **yq: `recurse(.[]?; cond)` walks cursors instead of materializing the document** (#4095). Under `--jq-extensions`, for a `cond` that yields at most one value, `succinctly yq` now takes the cursor walk jq mode took since #3867, where it built the whole alias-expanded document as an owned tree first. Over a 100 MB `users` document, `first(recurse(.[]?; true))` is 75.5% faster on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X and 73.6% on an Apple M4 Pro, collecting every node 58.0% and 50.2%, and `length > 0`, `type` tests and `. != null` conditions are 35.5% and 28.0%, 33.0% and 13.3%, and 21.1% and 5.6% faster. Every one of the 21 rows measured is faster on the 7950X; on the M4 Pro the only slower ones are a pair of comparisons joined by `and` (`. != null and . != false`, 3.9% to 4.7% slower). Over a 26-level alias ladder (2^26 leaves once expanded), `.root | first(recurse(.[]?; true))` takes 4.3 ms on the M4 Pro and 1.8 ms on the 7950X where it took 16.9 s and 37.8 s (and over 8 GB). In jq mode a bare `recurse(.[]?; . != null)` over a 10 MB JSON document is 62.9% and 54.9% faster, its collecting form unchanged. A comparison of `.` or `type` with a literal is decided without the general evaluator, which also stops a container being copied whole to be compared. Interleaved A/B against `c31f900b8` on both machines, output identical on every row.
