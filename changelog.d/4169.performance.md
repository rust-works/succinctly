- **jq: `to_entries`, `.[]`, `map_values`, `paths` and the printer's `[.]` / `{a: .}` over a wide object no longer sort every key hash to look for a repeated key** (#4169). They now use the bitset prefilter #3343 gave `length` (`any_hash_repeats`, 128 to 2^21 keys), which sorts only the few hashes that land on a bit another hash had set, so the answer is the same. Because both paths build the full field list first, the sort was a small share of their cost: on a 2 MB `wide` object (159 K keys) `to_entries | length` runs 1.7% fewer instructions and `[.]` 2.7% fewer on a 7950X (1.6% and 2.0% on an M4 Pro), and median wall-clock falls 0.3% to 3.1% from 159 K to 1.9 M keys on both, except `[.]` on the M4 Pro at 1.55 M to 1.77 M keys, which is flat. Arrays of 32- to 512-key objects stay within the measurement band (instructions -0.3% or less), and peak RSS is unchanged. `keys_unsorted` past 786 K keys keeps its sort: the prefilter was up to 4.1% slower there on the M4 Pro (1.55 M to 1.77 M keys) for at most about 2% elsewhere; see `docs/parsing/json.md`. Output is identical, error text and exit status included, across 742 comparisons.
