- **jq: an experimental call machine, behind `SUCCINCTLY_CALL_MACHINE=1`, off by default** (#7977, step 1 of its migration plan).
  A jq-mode program that holds a recursive `def` can run on a compiled stack machine in jq 1.7.1's image: instructions over contiguous data, frame and fork stacks with gojq's protection watermark, tail calls, and values moved out at their last read.
  The switch exists for measurement: one binary is timed with `=1` against `=0` before the default flips in a later step (#7961).
  With it on, the machine answers the 142 programs of its corpus (`scripts/call_machine/`) exactly as jq 1.7.1 does, among them linear recursion 10,000 deep that the shipped evaluators refuse at their native-stack floor, and #7803's memo copies its array once at N = 3,200, where jq copies it at every level.
  Until step 2 it has known gaps under `=1`: a loop that never deepens (`def f: f; f`, `def f($n): f($n); f(0)`) runs forever where the shipped evaluators refuse it, `[...]` accumulators and a leaf's buffered outputs are not counted against its memory budget, and its allocations are not fallible.
  With the switch unset or `0` nothing changes: the machine is never asked, so no program is compiled for it and no route moves.
  The library reads no environment for it: `succinctly jq` reads the variable and turns the machine on through `path_mode::set_call_machine`.
