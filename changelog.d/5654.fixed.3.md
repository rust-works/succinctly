- **jq: `arr | map(f)` and `obj | map(f)` refuse a bad gap they pull past** (#5654, #1641 residual). `[1@,2] | map(.)`, `[1,,2]`, `[1,2,]`, `[1 2]`, `{"a":1,"b":2,}` and `{"a":1,"b"}` answered at exit 0 because the lazy source read the elements through a cons list that ran no gap check; jq 1.7.1 refuses each as a parse error, and `succinctly jq` does now, with jq's messages. A consumer that stops early (`first(.[])`, `map(.) | first`) never pulls past its answer, so a bad gap after it is still not seen; matching jq there would validate the rest of the document for a filter that exists to stop early. Cost, retired instructions on an Apple M5 Max (min of 5, release, not wall-clock; x86_64 not measured): `map(.) | length` over 3M integers +9.9%, `map(.[0])` over short arrays +6.1%, an object of 1M members +6.4%, `map(.+1)` +2.2%, and `.users | map(.id)` / `map(select(...))` over 20 MB of records +1.8% / +0.7%; filters that do not pull through `map` are within 0.1%, apart from `.[]|select(...)` at +1.4% (code layout).
