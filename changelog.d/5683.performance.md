- **jq: `min` and `max` over an array walk it once and read a plain integer without decoding it** (#5683). `min`/`max` collected a cursor for every element, then keyed every element with its decoded value into a second vector, then compared; a comma of the two paid that twice. They now keep the running winner as they walk (the first of equal minima and the last of equal maxima, as before, so `1`, `1.0` and `1.00` are still told apart by their spelling), and an element that is a plain integer of up to 18 digits is compared as an `i64` instead of being built as an owned number. Everything else (a fraction, an exponent, `-0`, a longer run, a non-number) takes the general decode. Over a 700,000-number array (Apple M-series, interleaved min, output identical to jq's): `[.numbers|min,max]` 115.6 -> 35.9 ms (-69%), `[.numbers|min]` 66.3 -> 23.1 ms (-65%); at 5,000,000 numbers 826 -> 230 ms (-72%) and 451 -> 140 ms (-69%). jq / succinctly goes from 0.71x to 2.3x on the comma and from 1.02x to 2.9x on `min` alone. Only the walk's error order changed: an array with two faults reports the one first in the text. Measured on one machine; x86_64 was not.
