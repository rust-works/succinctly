- **jq: a fold whose UPDATE is a slice with literal bounds plus a closed operand (`.[1:] + [$i]`, a sliding queue) is linear** (#7109).
  `reduce range(N) as $i (.; .[1:] + [$i])` wrote the whole state out as JSON and indexed it again on every step, so
  it was quadratic: 35.3 s at N=20,000 on an Apple M4 Pro and 37.7 s on a Ryzen 9 7950X, against jq 1.7.1's 0.05 s.
  The step now cuts the owned state and combines it with the operand, as the piped form `.[1:] | . + [$i]` already
  did, and the same row takes 9.6 ms on both (5,000 and 10,000 steps: 1.97 s and 7.77 s on the M4 Pro, 2.24 s and
  9.06 s on the 7950X, before; 4.7 ms and 6.4 ms, 3.9 ms and 5.9 ms after). Measured interleaved with an A/A control
  (7 repetitions, 3 at 20,000; the control moved 3.6% at most), output identical, and the piped form and
  `. + [$i]` rows do not move.
  A slice with a computed bound, a slice on the right of the `+`, and a right operand that reads the state
  (`[.[0]]`) still take the slower route.
