- **jq: a body rebuilt for each value that reads its variable late is linear in its stage count, not quadratic** (#5441).
  #5436 keeps the tails of a rebuilt body that start after its last reading stage, so a body that reads its variable
  in its last stage (`. as $x | (. + 1 | ... | . + $x)`), second last or in the middle still copied its rest per
  value per stage. A tail of at least 8 stages that holds a reading stage is now handed on in pieces, as #5421 hands
  on one holding a `def` call: each run of plain arithmetic, comparison or literal stages between the reading stages
  is a copy the pipe keeps, and each reading stage is the rebuilt body's own. The same applies to a `reduce`/`foreach`
  update, a `|=` body, a destructuring bind and a `def f($a)` that reads `$a` late. `succinctly yq` is unchanged, and
  so is a body whose stages are not plain arithmetic (`.a`, `[.]`, a call): those still copy their tails per value.
  20,000 values, interleaved min of 9 CPU seconds, output identical, `[range(0;20000) | . as $x | (. + 1 | ... |
  . + $x)] | length`: n=14 0.372 s -> 0.120 s, n=40 2.373 s -> 0.394 s on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (the same pipe
  reading its variable first: 0.393 s); n=14 0.242 s -> 0.091 s, n=40 1.478 s -> 0.269 s on an Apple M4 Pro
  (first: 0.268 s). Read second last, in the middle, by a `reduce` / `foreach` / `|=` update, a destructuring bind,
  a `def f($a)` or over document input: 0.17x to 0.22x on the 7950X, 0.18x to 0.24x on the M4 Pro. A variable read
  in every stage, first, or not at all, n=6, and twelve record queries (`map`, `sort_by`, `group_by`, `select`,
  `to_entries`, `with_entries`, `reduce`, `paths`, `walk`, `tostring | fromjson`) are within 3% on both machines.
