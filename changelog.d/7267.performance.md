- **jq: an array of values computed per element of a document array holds a third to a half less memory** (#7267).
  `[.xs[] | f]` kept every element's result as an 80-byte evaluator result until the end of the walk, beside a
  second vector of the values that it grew by doubling, so it held 112-144 bytes per element where jq holds 16.
  Once the first 64 elements of a walk of at least 128 have each yielded one computed value or nothing, the values
  now go straight into one vector sized for the walk, which is the result; a shorter walk, or one that meets another
  kind of result first (a cursor, an inner generator's values, a failure), carries on exactly as before. At 100 MB, `[.numbers[]|floor]|add` peaks
  at 547 MiB instead of 1,081 MiB on an Apple M4 Pro and at 554 MiB instead of 874 MiB on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X,
  now below jq 1.7.1 on both, and runs 6% faster on the M4 Pro; `[.strings[]|length]|add` peaks at 158 MiB
  instead of 215 MiB (M4 Pro) and 164 MiB instead of 199 MiB (7950X), and `[.users[] | {name, s: (.score + 1)}]`
  at 349 MiB instead of 402 MiB (M4 Pro) and 408 MiB instead of 440 MiB (7950X). The output is unchanged.
