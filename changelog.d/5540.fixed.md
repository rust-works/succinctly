- **jq: a fold step that stores its loop variable by assignment or arithmetic hands on the element itself** (#5540).
  `[[nan]] as $a | reduce $a[] as $v (null; . + $v) == $a[0]` (jq's own definition of `add`), `reduce $a[] as $v ([]; . + [$v]) | .[0] == $a[0]`, `reduce $a as $v ({}; .x = $v) | .x == $a` and `.x |= $v` are `true` in jq and were `false` here (jq's `jv_equal` short-circuits on `jv_identical`, and `[nan] == [nan]` is `false`).
  #5516 left these steps out because they run in place on the owned accumulator, which declined a container marker and then copied the accumulator on every step.
  A fold's identity-sharing marker is the loop element's own storage, registered in no embed table, so the in-place step now stores it as one `Rc` clone and keeps running in place; a marker an `as` binding made still makes the step decline.
  Nothing rebuilds the element, so these folds got cheaper (interleaved A/B, 3.5 MB and 14 MB of records, output identical to the merge base on every row).
  On an Apple M5 Max (release, instructions retired): `reduce .users[] as $r ([]; . + [$r])` -35% / -36%, `.[$r.name] = $r` -34% / -34%, `. + {($r.name): $r}` -32% / -32%, `reduce .users[] as $r (null; . + $r)` -24% / -24%, and the same two stores under `. as $d |` -24% / -25%.
  On an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (release, min of 7 wall-clock): -20% / -19%, -20% / -19%, -18% / -16%, -24% / -24%, and -12% / -14% under `. as $d |`.
  The folds that store only a scalar field (`.[$r.name] = $r.score`, `. + $r.score`, `. + [$r.id]`) moved within -1.2% to +0.7% on both.
  `reduce f(400) as $x ([]; . + [$x]) | length` over a value built 400 levels deep now answers `1`, as jq does, instead of #5526's `nesting depth exceeds limit of 384` refusal. `succinctly yq` is unchanged.
