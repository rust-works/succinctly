- **jq: lookahead, lookbehind, backreferences and atomic groups in a regex answer as in jq** (#5502). `gsub("(?=u)"; "u")`, `[splits("(?=[A-Z])")]`, `sub("(?<=\\d)(?=(\\d{3})+(?!\\d))"; ","; "g")`, `test("(ab)\\1")` and patterns with `(?>…)`, `\k<name>`, `(?(1)…|…)`, `\K`, `\G`, `\R` or `\e` failed with `invalid regex`, because the `regex` crate has none of them. A pattern it refuses is now compiled by `fancy-regex` (a new dependency, pure Rust), but only when every construct and escape in it was checked against jq to mean the same; `\h`, `\g<name>`, `(?~…)` and the like keep the error. Patterns the `regex` crate accepts are unchanged, and yq mode, whose dialect is Go's, still refuses all of these as yq does. A search that runs out of backtracking budget fails with jq's `Regex failure: retry-limit-in-match over`; the two engines run out on different patterns, and a few lookbehinds jq refuses are answered (both in `limitations.md`).
