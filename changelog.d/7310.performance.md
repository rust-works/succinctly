- **jq: `.` and `-c .` over a wide object that repeats a key take 20-29% less time** (#7310).
  The canonical scan that lets a document be printed from its text settles a wide object's key hashes at its
  closing brace, after every member is certified, so a repeated key is all it has left to decline, and it has
  hashed every key on the way. The printer then walked the keys again to hash and sort them before collapsing the
  repeat (#7311). It now takes the hashes the scan found repeated and skips that walk. On the 100 MB `wide` object
  (7.1 M keys) with one repeated key, `jq .` takes 0.96 s instead of 1.34 s and `jq -c .` 0.94 s instead of
  1.32 s on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, and 0.75 s instead of 1.02 s for both on an Apple M4 Pro, which retires
  17,500 M instructions instead of 23,277 M (`.`) and 16,905 M instead of 22,682 M (`-c .`) and peaks at 313 MiB
  instead of 370 MiB. The saving is 20-22% at 10 MB, and where the repeat sits does not matter.
