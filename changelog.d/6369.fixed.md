- **yq: a block scalar that holds U+0085, U+2028 or U+2029 is read as go-yaml reads it** (#6369). go-yaml ends a block scalar's line at each of the three as at `\n`, so the spaces after one are indentation (`a: |` over `  x<U+2028>  y` is `"x y\n"`, where `succinctly yq` kept the two spaces), a line left of the indent after one ends the scalar (`  x<U+2028>b: 1` is the scalar `"x "` and a key `b`, where the whole line was content), a comment, key, sequence item or `---` there is structure or an error (`  x<U+2028>y` and `  x<U+2028>- y` are refused), and U+0085 is a `\n` in the value while U+2028 and U+2029 stay and never fold in a `>` scalar. The scalar's lines, its end, its indent and its value are read that way for exactly the scalars that hold one of the three; the parser notes one on the line walk it already makes (looking at a byte only where a `0xC2` or `0xE2` lead byte shows up) and lists the scalar in the index, so the decoders never scan for them. This replaces #6287's tab-after-break check, which is a case of the same reading. Checked against yq v4.53.3 on 960 documents (four headers, the three breaks, 0-3 spaces on each side, five tails) and on 40,000 random ones (seventeen headers: `|`, `>`, `-`, `+`, explicit indents, `&anchor`, `!!str`, sequence items and a nested key; leading, trailing and empty lines made of spaces and any break, CRLF and lone CR; a dedent line or none), with no difference. The random draws put no break after a line left of the scalar's indent, since three neighbouring shapes outside a block scalar still differ: a plain key that spans a break, a comment line that ends at one, and a document-root `--- |` whose first blank line is spaces and a NEL (#6820). Time, interleaved and min of 11 (medians quoted) on a block-scalar corpus with no break at 10 and 50 MB: AMD Ryzen 9 7950X -2.0% to +0.4% on ASCII and -1.5% to +0.8% on text with U+2014 and U+00E9, 5-8% faster on text with tabs; Apple M4 Pro -1.2% to +1.3% on both (control -3.3% to +2.2%) and 1.5-9% faster on text with tabs. A rescan of the scalar for the three breaks, tried first, cost 12-18% on the non-ASCII text, which is why the walk looks at a byte only where a lead byte shows up. A scalar that does hold a break takes the slower path: 14,240 block scalars that each hold an LS index in 28.0 ms against 14.6 ms (7950X, 10 MB).
