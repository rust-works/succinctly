- **jq: a `reduce`/`foreach` step that builds its next state with a constructor is 2.0-2.7x faster** (#6528).
  A step such as `[.[0] + $x, .[1] + 1]` or `{s: (.s + $x), n: (.n + 1)}`, with no bare `.` member, now
  builds the new state from the old one directly. Before, it went through the general evaluator and
  re-indexed the state for every `.[0]` it read. Over 800k floats on an Apple M5 these folds went from
  0.35x-0.49x of jq 1.7.1 to 0.90x-1.24x, the same at 1, 10 and 100 MB: `reduce .[] as $x ([0, 0];
  [.[0] + $x, .[1] + 1])` takes 3.2 s at 100 MB instead of 8.1 s (jq 3.7 s). A fold-step operand that
  reads a finite float off the state, or does arithmetic or division on one (`.a += [.f + 1]`), is now
  read off the tree as integers already were, so that fold is linear: 10,000 steps take 6 ms instead
  of 2.7 s, and 160,000 take 60 ms (jq 1.7.1: 93 ms and 18 s).
