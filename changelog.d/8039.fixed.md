- **yq: `key` and `parent` inside `with(PATH; . |= f)`, and inside any update nested in an update's filter, read the target's position** (#8039).
  `with(.b; . |= key)` is `.b |= (. |= key)`, and yq writes `"b"` there as it does for `.b |= key`, but the nested update answered no `key` at its own input, so `{"a":1,"b":5}` came back unchanged; `.b | . |= key` answered `5` for yq's `"b"`, and `with(.a.b; . |= (parent | keys))` left the node where yq writes the keys of `.a`. `key` is now the last component of the position the enclosing update installed, and `parent` climbs that update's input, so nested `with`, array members (`with(.a[]; . |= key)` is `[0,1,2]`), a missing target and `--eval-all`/`-i` agree. An update whose own reads cannot be resolved at its target now names that target for the updates nested in its filter, so `.x |= (.b |= (.c | . |= path))` is `["x","b","c"]` where it was `["x","c"]`. Whether a value the filter builds has a position follows the rule a read already uses, so `.x |= (map(.) | (. |= path))` and `.a |= (.b |= (5 | . |= path))` are `[]` and `.x |= ({} | .k | (. |= path))` is `["k"]`, as in yq. Two rows now follow that rule where it differs from yq and main matched by accident: `.x |= (.b |= (.b.c + 1 | . |= key))` writes `"c"` (yq leaves `1`) and `.x |= (splitDoc | (. |= (parent | keys)))` writes `["x"]` (yq leaves `.x`), as `.x | .b | (.b.c + 1 | key)` and `.x | splitDoc | (parent | keys)` already answered. `with(.b; . |= (select(false) | key))` still leaves the input unchanged. `succinctly jq` is unchanged: `with` is not a jq builtin, and a nested update there still climbs no `parent`. Rows captured from yq v4.53.3. A sweep of 5,114 `with`/`|=` bodies against yq and main fixed 1,444 rows; 5 rows broke, all of them the two identity-rule rows above; 52 changed from one wrong answer to another (the `parent` vivification divergence #2435, `(.zz, .b)` and `.[0]` on a mapping).
