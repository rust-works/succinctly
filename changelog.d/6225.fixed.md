- **jq: Oniguruma's multi-character case folding is read under the `i` flag** (#6225). `"SS" | test("ß";"i")`, `"STRASSE" | test("straße";"i")` and `"ﬁ" | test("fi";"i")` were `false`: the `regex` crate folds one character to one, where Oniguruma folds with the whole of `CaseFolding.txt` (`ß` is `ss`, `ﬁ` is `fi`, `ﬃ` is `ffi`, `ŉ` is `ʼn`, the Greek iota subscripts). The pattern is now rewritten to the expansion Oniguruma makes of it, a port of `unravel_case_fold_string` over the library's own fold tables (v6.9.8, Unicode 14): a run of literals is walked from the left, the longest fold found there (three characters, then two, then one) becomes an alternation, and the walk goes on after the characters it covered, so `sss` finds `ßs` and not `sß`, as in jq. A bracketed class holding a character that folds to several (`[ß]`, `[ß-ÿ]`, `[\w]`) gains the alternative that spells it; a negated class and a look-behind do not. Checked against jq 1.7.1 over 60,000 random patterns and subjects. Limits are listed in `docs/compliance/jq/limitations.md`.
