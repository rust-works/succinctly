- **jq: `flatten($depth)` and `range($from; $upto; $by)` compare a number literal too small for a double with `0` as jq does** (#6993).
  jq defines both in jq, and two number literals compare exactly as decimals there: `[[[[1]]]] | flatten(1e-1000)` is `[1]` (the depth is not `0`, and its `- 1` is a computed `-1`, which never is), `flatten` of a depth read as `-1e-400` raises `flatten depth must not be negative`, and `[limit(3; range(0; 1; 1e-1000))]` is `[0,0,0]` (a positive step that adds `0.0`), where `succinctly jq` answered `[[[[1]]]]`, `[[[[1]]]]` and `[]`.
  `range/3`'s first `$from < $upto` reads its literals too, so `[range(0; 1e-1000; 1)]` is `[0]` and `[range(0.1; 0.10000000000000000001; 1)]` is `[0.1]`, as in jq; `range/1` and `range/2`, which jq computes in C over doubles, are unchanged, as is `succinctly yq`.
