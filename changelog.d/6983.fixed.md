- **jq: `limit`, `nth` and `abs` compare a number literal too small for a double with `0` as jq does** (#6983).
  `jq -nc '[limit(1e-1000; 1, 2)]'` is `[1]`, and `succinctly jq` answered `[]`: jq's `$n > 0`, `$n == 0` and `. < 0` compare two number literals exactly, so `1e-1000` is above `0` although its double is `0.0`, and `-1e-400` is below it (`limit` passes every output through, `nth` raises `nth doesn't support negative indices`, and `abs` negates it to a computed `0` where it printed `-1E-400`).
  The same holds for a literal read from the document, `--argjson` or `tonumber`; a computed number (`1e-1000 * 1`, or `-1e-400` written in the program, which jq negates) is a double and still zero, as in jq. `succinctly yq` reads such a count by its double, as before.
