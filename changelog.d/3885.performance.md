- **jq: a long arithmetic chain no longer re-walks itself once per operator** (#3885). Each binary operator asked `settles_before_consumer` of its left operand first, and refusing an operand that calls no `def` means walking all of it, so in `1 + 1 + ... + 1` every one of N operators re-walked everything below it (N² node visits) to refuse a right operand that is one literal. The operator now probes the right operand with an 8-node budget first and the left only if that cannot decide (`settles_both`); an operand the probe refuses ends the question on either side, and which operands settle is unchanged. `map(<N terms>)` over 20,000 numbers on an Apple M-series, `succinctly jq` user time against the commit before, interleaved: 50 terms 0.30 s → 0.15 s, 100 terms 1.01 s → 0.29 s, 200 terms 3.95 s → 0.60 s (about 2x per doubling, was 4x); `succinctly yq` 100 terms 1.11 s → 0.33 s. A right-nested chain (`(.*1) + ((.*1) + ...)`), a chain of `def` calls, `sum_to(800)` and `fib(18)` are within ±2%. Output identical.
