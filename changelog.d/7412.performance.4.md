- **jq/yq on Linux: 0.5-1 MB less resident binary in every run** (#7412).
  Three things kept pages of the executable itself in the RSS of every run. The relocation table
  (`.rela.dyn`, 429 KiB), which the loader reads once before `main`, stayed mapped to the end; it is
  now unmapped first thing in `main` (`MADV_DONTNEED` on its whole pages, which are clean pages of
  the read-only segment). The C runtime's `_start` and `crtbeginS.o` functions were left out of the
  link order, 2 MB past the ordered block, and on Linux 6.18 the page they share kept a large folio of
  text resident; the linker wrapper now puts them first. And the functions the windowed stream routes
  run were unordered, because the order was traced at 1 MB and those routes start at 4 MiB; the 60 of
  them are now at the end of the list. Peak RSS on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X: `jq -n 1` 4.31 to 3.77 MiB,
  `.` over the npm corpus file 4.82 to 4.29, `-r .name` over 100 MB of NDJSON 5.72 to 4.81, `--seq -r
  .name` 5.82 to 5.03, and about 0.9 MiB less on every 100 MB row. On an Apple M4 Pro only the stream
  rows change, by 0.05-0.1 MiB. Times are unchanged within an A/A control and the spread that inert
  padding alone produces, on both machines.
