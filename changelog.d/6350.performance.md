- **jq, yq: `select(cond)` answers a simple condition without copying the node** (#6350). `select` over `true`/`false`/`null`, `.`, `not`, `and`/`or` and a comparison of `.` or `type` with a literal (`select(. == null)`, `select(. != null)`, `select(type == "string")`) now reads the node in place, as `recurse(.[]?; cond)` has since #4095, instead of running the condition through the fan-out and copying the node to compare it. `[.. | select(. == null)]` over a 50 MB document: 2.41 s to 1.10 s on an Apple M4 Pro (2.19x), `. != null` 2.50 s to 1.16 s, `. == true` 2.49 s to 1.13 s, `select(type=="string")` 1.70 s to 1.12 s (1.51x); flat from 1 to 50 MB, interleaved, min of 7, the control `[.. | strings]` 1.00x. On an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X the same programs are 2.39x (`== null`), 2.26x (`!= null`), 2.39x (`== true`) and 1.61x (`type=="string"`) at 50 MB. The answers are unchanged, with one exception: a container compared with a literal (`select(. != null)` over `[[1,tru],2]`) is decided by being one and is no longer decoded, so malformed JSON nested inside it is not reported there, as it already was not for `select(type == "array")` or `select(.)`.
