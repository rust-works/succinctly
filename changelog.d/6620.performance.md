- **jq: a `--seq` stream with a diagnostic reads its plain records in one pass** (#6620).
  One truncated record sends a `--seq` run down the route that converts each record as the queue is read, and
  52% of its time on a 100 MB stream was building each record through the checked materializer (an index, a
  delimiter walk, a cursor conversion). A record of plain RFC 8259 text is now read directly, as `fromjson`
  and `--slurpfile` read it; anything the one-pass reader declines (jq's leniencies, a repeated key, nesting
  past 128 levels, a grammar error) is decided by the materializer as before. `--seq -r .name` over 100 MB with
  one truncated record went from 3.33 s to 2.39 s (first record) and 3.37 s to 2.43 s (last), and `--seq -c .`
  from 2.96 s to 1.88 s and 3.03 s to 1.92 s, which is 1.14-1.17x of jq 1.7.1 (2.19 s); `.name` and `.id`
  stay at 0.45-0.48x of it (jq: 1.10 s) (AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, interleaved, median of 5). A clean stream is
  unchanged (0.99-1.01x).
