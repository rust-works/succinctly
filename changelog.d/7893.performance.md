- **jq: the outputs of a recursive generator reach their consumer from where they are produced, not through every level of the recursion** (#7893).
  `def f: if . > 0 then (. - 1 | f), . else . end; [N | f] | length` handed each output of a level back up through every level above it, N²/2 calls for its N outputs, where jq hands each straight to the consumer.
  Two things stood between a level's call and the sink its caller was given.
  Since #7856 every call to a def that may recurse wrapped its sink in a check of the native stack, even where the sink was already such a check: that was the whole cost of `def f($n): if $n > 0 then f($n - 1), $n else $n end; [f(N)]`, in `succinctly yq --jq-extensions` too.
  And since #7702 a def that threads its count through `.` ran its `,` and its `. - 1 | f` on the owned evaluator's re-index bridge, whose own sink the call was given.
  A call whose sink is the check of the call that handed it on is no longer wrapped again (each output is still checked, once), and a `,` or a pipe that ends in the recursive call runs on the owned tree with the sink it was given.
  Under cachegrind on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (x86_64 Linux, release build) the first program retires 35.8 / 70.3 / 139.5 million instructions at N = 1,500 / 3,000 / 6,000 where it retired 126.7 / 416.3 / 1,488.7 million, and the second 40.4 / 79.6 / 158.0 million where it retired 68.6 / 192.2 / 608.2 million; `reduce`, `foreach`, `first`, `last`, `limit` and `select` as the consumer scale the same way.
  The answers are unchanged, and so is every refusal #7856 added: a recursion whose outputs climb through a consumer at each level is checked at each level, as before.
