- **jq: a loop with a `$` way under `(?m)` answers as Oniguruma's does, and so does one with a `^` way when no way ends a line** (#6425). `"a\nb" | [match("(?m)($|a|\\n|b)*";"g") | .string]` printed `["a\nb",""]` and jq 1.7.1 answers `["a","","b",""]`: under `(?m)` `$` holds before every newline, so the empty iteration that ends Oniguruma's loop was dropped by the `regex` crate, which went on to consume the newline. A way that starts with a newline runs only where `$` holds and any other only where it does not, which tells the two kinds of head apart without a complement of the anchor: the loop is written as the second kind, greedy, then the first, lazy, each leaving through the empty way. A `^` holds again after a newline a way ends with, so only a body no way of which ends one is written, as `\A` is. A way that may start either way (`\s`, `[^b]`), a `^` beside a way that ends a line, a `(?m)` that something switches, and the `n` flag keep their text and differ. Over three draws of 3,000 loops under `(?m)` the patterns that differ fall from 273, 260 and 255 to 235, 229 and 219 and two in all changed the other way, each on a subject that ends with a newline, where `(?m)^` holds here and not in jq (#6456).
