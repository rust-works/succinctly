- **jq: `-n` programs that read `input`/`inputs`, and `-R`, read a regular file a window at a time, so their peak memory no longer grows with the file** (#7023, slice 2 of #6920). `jq -n 'reduce inputs as $x (...)'` and `jq -R length` over a file used to hold all of it: 107 MiB (aarch64) and 115 MiB (x86_64) at 100 MiB of NDJSON, where jq's stays at a few MB. They now hold about a 1 MiB window and the line or document being run, 11 to 16 MiB on both machines, flat from 10 MiB to 100 MiB. The `inputs` route also takes standard input this way. `-R` takes a regular file of 4 MiB or more; a file that is smaller, several files, and standard input under `-R` are read whole as before. Output, error wording, exit status, `input_line_number` and the `(at file:N)` marker after the last input are the whole-file route's, checked against it and against jq 1.7.1 on faults at several positions, an unterminated last document, a BOM, invalid UTF-8, documents that run across lines, a last line with no line break and a document larger than a window. Time on a 7950X at 100 MiB: `reduce inputs` −2.7%, `-R` with a filter −6.0%, `-nR` with `reduce inputs` −54%; on an M4 Pro +0.4%, −2.0% and −60%. At 1 MiB `reduce inputs` reads +5% slower on the 7950X (0.5 ms) and +1% on the M4 Pro.
