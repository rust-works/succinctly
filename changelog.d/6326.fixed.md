- **jq: a global regex search steps one byte after an empty match, so a multibyte character gives an empty match per continuation byte, as jq 1.7.1's does** (#6326). `"ß" | [match("";"g") | .offset]` printed `[0,1]`; jq answers `[0,1,1]`, and `"ß" | gsub("";"X")` is `"XßXX"` in jq and was `"XßX"`, `"ß" | [splits("")]` is `["","ß","",""]`. The extra matches are empty, at the next character's offset, with the same empty captures; they follow a pattern with no assertion (`""`, `x*`, `\s*`, `a?`, groups and alternations of them), and the `n` flag still drops them. Over 2,100 generated patterns on strings of one- to four-byte characters, 1,114 differed from jq before and 2 do now; a pattern with `\b`, `^`, `$` or a look-around keeps the character step (`docs/compliance/jq/limitations.md`).
