- **jq: `strptime`'s `%d` and `%e` read the white space before the day as jq's libc does** (#5974). A white-space character stands in for the first of the day's two digits: `%d` reads `" 5"` and `"\t5"` as day 5 (they were no match) and refuses `"  5"` and `" 05"`, and `%e` skips one white-space character and then reads as `%d` does, so `"\t5"` and `"  5"` are day 5 (the first was no match) and `"   5"` is no match (it was day 5). A day above 31 is refused where it is read (`"500" | strptime("%d%d")` read 50 first and answered), and `%D`, `%F`, `%c` and `%x`, which stand for `%d`, read their day the same way. Checked against jq 1.7.1 on macOS over 93,600 rows (20 formats, every string of up to four characters from white space, digits, `-` and `/`): every row that does not read a `%Y` or `%y` from an empty field now matches, 11,826 differed before. glibc's `strptime` skips any run of white space, so jq on Linux reads more of these than the pin does; this follows the pin.
