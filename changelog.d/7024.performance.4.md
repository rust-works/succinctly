- **jq: `-R` over standard input reads it a window at a time, so its peak memory no longer grows with the stream** (#7024, #7023). `cat log | jq -R .` (a pipe or socket holding 4 MiB or more) and `jq -R . < log` (a regular file with 4 MiB or more left from where it stands) read the whole stream first, as `-R` over a file named on the command line did before #7023; they are now read by that route's `LineWindows`, a regular file from its offset and a pipe after a probe of its first 4 MiB. A stream of lines has no fault to word, so nothing is read again or certified. Measured interleaved at 100 MiB of NDJSON against the route that read it whole, with an A/A control (within 3.6%) and outputs identical, on idle machines: peak RSS falls from 111 to 115 MiB to 14 to 20 MiB on a Ryzen 9 7950X and from 107 to 108 MiB to 8 to 13 MiB on an M4 Pro, and the run is faster on a pipe, `-R .` by 7.5% and 5.4%, `-nR 'reduce inputs as $l (0; .+1)'` by 15.8% and 6.8% and `-R 'select(endswith("9}"))'` by 11.8% and 6.7%, and redirected by 0.9% to 16.3%. A standard input not opened for reading (`jq . 0>>file`) reads as empty at every size again, on this route and on the plain filter's pipe and redirected-file routes, which had reported `EBADF` at 4 MiB or more. A pipe under 4 MiB, a terminal, `--slurp`, `--stream`, `--seq` and `--validate` still read the input whole, and a run that stops early now leaves the rest of the pipe unread (and a redirected file at the offset its last window ended), as jq does.
