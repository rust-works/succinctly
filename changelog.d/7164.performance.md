- **jq: `--seq` over a pipe on standard input is read a window at a time, so its peak memory no longer grows with the stream** (#7164, the last route of #7024). `cat records.seq | jq --seq -c .` over a pipe or socket holding 4 MiB or more read the whole stream: 125 MiB at 100 MiB of RFC 7464 records on a Ryzen 9 7950X and 123 MiB on an M4 Pro, where a regular file has been flat since #7082. Each record is now certified when the RS after it (or the stream's end) is read, with the check the whole-file route makes, and the windows of certified records are read with each RS as a space: 15 to 17 MiB and 11 to 12 MiB at 100 MiB, and 2.8% to 4.2% faster on both machines (`-c .`, `-r .name`, `select`; interleaved, with an A/A control within 0.7%). A pipe cannot be read twice and output cannot be taken back, so at the first record that is not `RS value LF` (or one over 64 MiB) the windows stop before it and the rest of the pipe is read whole by the route that walks jq's reader, resumed where jq's reader stood after the records already run (the line, the `fgets` chunks and the stale byte of jq's token buffer that a bare `n` reads). Every warning, value, `(at <stdin>:N)` line and the end-of-stream drop come out as for the stream read whole. A stream that turns only near its end now runs its clean prefix on the lazy route instead of the materializing one: 149 to 15 MiB and 53% faster on the 7950X, 153 to 11 MiB and 58% faster on the M4 Pro. Checked against the same pipe read whole by unit tests of the resumed reader over 4,000 generated streams, by a test of the gate over every read size and probe cut, by CLI tests pinned to jq 1.7.1, and by 1,230 cases of `scripts/jq-windowed-file-fuzz.py --pipe --seq` with no difference. A pipe under 4 MiB, `-s`, `--validate` and `-n` still read the stream whole.
