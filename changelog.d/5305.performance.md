- **jq: a string interpolation of plain navigation reads its `\(...)` slots at the cursor** (#5305). `.users[] | "\(.name) <\(.email)>"` ran at 0.8x jq on 100 MB while the equivalent `.name + " <" + .email + ">"` ran at 1.7x: the evaluator copied the whole ambient value into an owned one (serialised and indexed it again) to read a field of it, so every user record was rebuilt per output. An interpolation whose slots are all plain navigation (`.`, a field or index, `.[]`, `?`, parentheses and pipes of those) now reads them off the document node itself, in the streamed (`.users[] | "..."`), collected (`[.users[] | "..."]`, `map("...")`) and object-value (`{n: "..."}`) forms; any other slot (a call, `..`, `del(.x)`, a position read, `inputs`) keeps the earlier route unchanged. Measured on an Apple M4 Pro and an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (release builds, interleaved, min of 9, output identical, 2 / 10 / 100 MB `users`): `.users[] | "\(.name)"` 1.34 s → 0.42 s on 100 MB (M4 Pro) and 1.62 s → 0.48 s (7950X), `.users[] | "\(.name) <\(.email)>"` 1.65 s → 0.74 s and 1.94 s → 0.79 s, `[.users[] | "\(.name)"] | length` 1.32 s → 0.39 s and 1.57 s → 0.50 s, `.users | map("\(.name) <\(.email)>") | length` 1.59 s → 0.69 s and 1.79 s → 0.69 s; the five-slot `"\(.id): \(.name) \(.age) \(.active) \(.score)"` is 34% and 40% faster, and the ratio is flat from 2 MB to 100 MB. The concatenation and `.name` controls are unchanged on the M4 Pro (within 1.3%) and read +2-5% on the 7950X at instruction counts identical to within 650 of 791M (code layout).
