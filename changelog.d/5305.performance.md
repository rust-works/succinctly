- **jq: a string interpolation reads its `\(...)` slots at the cursor** (#5305). `.users[] | "\(.name)"` ran at 0.8x jq while `.users[] | .name + ""` ran at 1.7x: the evaluator copied the whole ambient value into an owned one (serialised and indexed it again) to read a field of it, so every user record was rebuilt per output. A slot that reads no position is now evaluated against the document node itself, in the streamed (`.users[] | "..."`), collected (`[.users[] | "..."]`, `map("...")`) and object-value (`{n: "..."}`) forms. Measured on an Apple M4 Pro and an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X (release builds, interleaved, min of 9, output identical): `.users[] | "\(.name)"` 1.34 s → 0.41 s on 100 MB (M4 Pro) and 1.62 s → 0.46 s (7950X), `.users[] | "\(.name) <\(.email)>"` 1.61 s → 0.73 s and 1.92 s → 0.74 s, `[.users[] | "\(.name)"] | length` 1.32 s → 0.38 s and 1.56 s → 0.48 s; the five-slot `"\(.id): \(.name) \(.age) \(.active) \(.score)"` is 37-43% faster, and the `+` concatenation and `.name` controls are unchanged. An interpolation now validates only what its slots read, as `.name` and `[.name]` already did: it no longer raises over a malformed sibling field it never touches (see `docs/compliance/jq/limitations.md`).
