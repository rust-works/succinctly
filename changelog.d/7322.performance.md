- **jq: a `--seq` stream is read a window at a time past a bad record** (#7322). A `--seq` file of
  4 MiB or more (or a pipe) was windowed only up to its first record other than `RS value LF`, and
  the rest was held whole, so a bad first record cost the whole file. The windows now carry on when
  plain records follow the bad one: the stretch between is read as jq's reader reads it, its
  values run and its warnings are printed where jq prints them. `--seq -r .name` over 100 MiB whose
  first record is cut short (`seq_field_bad_first`) went from 979.4 to 703.0 ms (-28.2%) and 145.9
  to 10.1 MiB peak RSS on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, and from 908.1 to 683.8 ms (-24.7%) and 152.3 to
  6.6 MiB on an Apple M4 Pro; at 10 MiB it is -27.9% and -24.0%, and at 1 MiB (under the 4 MiB the
  windows start at) unchanged. A cut record every 1000 records reads the same (146.8 to 10.1 MiB
  and -31.0% on the 7950X, 152.6 to 6.7 MiB and -24.9% on the M4 Pro), as does the same stream on
  a pipe (146.3 to 13.2 MiB, 151.7 to 9.4 MiB). Output is identical, and identical to jq 1.7.1's.
  The rest of the stream is still held from a record the windows cannot carry on after: one jq
  reads a value from that is not RFC 8259 (`nan`, a control character in a string), a stretch of
  4 MiB or more or with over 1024 warnings, the stream's last record, and a bad record in any of
  several files.
