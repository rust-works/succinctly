- **jq: `first(inputs)` over a file no longer scans 64 KiB before it hands out the first record** (#7264, refs #7260). The lazy input scan started on a 64 KiB first chunk and a 256 KiB read of the file, each document checked by two parsers against each other (about 3,600 instructions per 85-byte record): 2.9 M of the 3.9 M instructions of `-n 'first(inputs)|.id'`, where `-n 1` is 1.1 M. The first chunk is now 4 KiB and doubles to the unchanged 512 KiB cap, and a file longer than the 256 KiB front is first read on 16 KiB (reading on to the front when the first chunk does not fit, and the rest of the front as the first window, so later windows fall where they did). `-n 'first(inputs)|.id'` over an NDJSON file takes 1.015 ms against 1.236 ms on a Ryzen 9 7950X (-15%, jq 1.7.1 1.071 ms; 1.35 M instructions against 3.94 M, callgrind) and 1.84 ms against 1.95 ms on an Apple M4 Pro (-6%; jq 1.55 ms), flat from 1 MB to 100 MB, and `-n 'input|.id'` 1.29 ms against 1.75 ms (7950X) and 2.15 ms against 2.61 ms (M4 Pro) over 10 MB. Interleaved A/B of 3,000 `posix_spawn` runs with an A/A control, output identical to the old binary and to jq 1.7.1. `reduce inputs` over the same files is within 0.3-0.7% at 10 and 100 MB on both machines and 1.6% (0.18 ms of 11 ms) slower at 1 MB on the 7950X, neutral on the M4 Pro; `-n 1`, eleven `--arg`/`--argjson` and `yq -n 1` startup do not move.
