- **`succinctly jq` reads a quantifier that follows a quantifier as jq's Oniguruma does** (#7682). Only a `?` or `+` right after a quantifier is its lazy or possessive marker; any further quantifier is a new one on the quantified atom, which Oniguruma reduces with the one before (`a??+` is `a*?`). `"aaa" | [match("(a)??++"; "gn")] | length` is `0` and `"aaab" | test("^(?:a|aa){2}+b")` is `false`, as in jq 1.7.1 and 1.8.2, where the first was refused (`possessive quantifier is not supported`) and the second `true`: such a run is now written as the tree Oniguruma builds, one quantifier per item, so a lazy quantifier followed by `+`, a quantifier after a possessive one, a possessive `{n}+` on an item that matches in several ways and a zero count over a quantified group all match as in jq. Over 37,067 generated rows the differences from jq 1.7.1 went from 2,567 to 1,401. `"aaaaaa" | [match("b|a{2,}?{2}"; "l")]` still differs, the `l` flag's documented no-op on a lazy pattern.
