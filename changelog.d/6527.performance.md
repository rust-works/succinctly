- **jq: `add`, `min` and `max` of an array that an earlier stage built out of numbers no longer go through the reindex bridge** (#6527). `.numbers | map(. * 2) | add`, `| max` and `| min` (and `[.numbers[] | . * 2] | add`) wrote the array out as JSON text (a shortest-round-trip float each), indexed it, and parsed every number back, 150 to 160 ms over 800,000 floats that jq 1.7.1 does not spend (0.64x to 0.75x of it on an Apple M5). The array is now folded where it stands, with the bridged builtins' own `arith_add`, `jq_min_by` and `jq_max_by`, so the answers are the same, including which of `1` and `1.0` a `max` keeps. An array holding anything but integers and finite floats (a NaN, an infinity, a number copied from the document, a string) still takes the bridge, as do an empty array, an object, yq mode and a run with a bound node in scope. Interleaved A/B (min of 11, output identity gated on all 27 configurations) over 1, 10 and 100 MB: `.numbers | map(. * 2) | add`, `| max` and `| min` are 38-40% faster on a Ryzen 9 7950X (10 MB: 421 to 255 ms) and 39-43% faster on an Apple M4 Pro (10 MB: 392 to 231 ms), `[.numbers[] | . * 2] | add` 37-38% and 40-43%, and the `length` and `sort | length` controls move by under 2%. A differential of 870 program-by-array rows against `/usr/bin/jq` is clean before and after.
