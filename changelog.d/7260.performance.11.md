- **jq: an object of up to 16 fields is a plain field list, and records share the keys they repeat** (#7260).
  An object was an `IndexMap` behind a refcount: an entries block, a separate hash-table block and an 88-byte
  box, so a six-field record cost three allocations beyond its strings. An object of up to 16 fields is now
  a plain list of its fields scanned in place, with a hash index only past 16, behind a 40-byte box; the JSON
  reader shares a key with the same key of the record before it, and `to_entries` builds every entry from two
  shared keys. At 10 MB of the `users` input, `.users[0].name="x"` peaks at 56 MB instead of 83 MB on an AMD
  Ryzen 9 7950X (jq 1.7.1: 65 MB) and 56 MB instead of 72 MB on an Apple M4 Pro (jq: 67 MB), 30% and 22%
  faster; `with_entries(.value+=1)` over a 10 MB object peaks at 480 MB instead of 828 MB (jq: 711 MB) and
  421 MB instead of 688 MB (jq: 898 MB), 32% and 20% faster; `--slurpfile` of 10 MB of NDJSON 38% and 37%
  faster. Library users: `OwnedValue::as_object()` now returns `Option<&FieldMap<OwnedValue>>` instead of an `IndexMap`; `FieldMap` keeps `IndexMap`'s order
  and duplicate-key behaviour.
