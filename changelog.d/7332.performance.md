- **jq: a run keeps its last value's index instead of freeing it first** (#7332). The process ends
  after the last value, and `free` of the index's buffers is the first large block glibc sees go
  back once the program has dropped a big array of small strings: when those buffers join one another
  past 64 KiB, it first sweeps every small chunk in its fast bins (`malloc_consolidate`), 54 M
  instructions and 3.2 M extra L1 misses for the million number spellings of `.numbers | sort |
  .[0]`. Whether the buffers sit side by side decided it, and #7151, which only changed the
  start-up allocations, flipped it: 331 to 355 ms at flat instructions. On a 7950X (min of 11
  interleaved runs, output identical) `.numbers | sort | .[0]` on `numbers-10mb` reads 345.3 to
  316.0 ms (-8.5%) and is 5.7 to 7.0% faster (min) with the heap start moved by 24, 40, 56 and 72
  bytes, 1 MB reads 28.4 to 27.3 ms and 100 MB 4,397 to 3,625 ms (-17.6%); instructions fall 1.1% (5,056 M to
  5,002 M). On an M4 Pro (macOS malloc) 10 MB and 100 MB read 230.9 and 230.3 ms, 2,668 and
  2,667 ms: no change. Only the run's last value keeps its index, so a stream of values still frees
  each one's.
