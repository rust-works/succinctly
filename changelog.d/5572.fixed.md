- **jq: `$` before a final newline picks the right match, and works after a doubled newline** (#5572). jq's `$` matches at the end of the text or just before a final `\n`; the text is searched with and without that `\n` and one match is picked. A tie went to the shorter match, so the trailing-whitespace idiom `"abc \n" | sub("\\s+$";"")` answered `"abc\n"` where jq answers `"abc"` (greedy `\s+` takes the `\n`, lazy `\s+?` does not), and a text ending in `"\n\n"` skipped the second search altogether (`"a\n\n" | [match("$";"g").offset]` was `[3]`, jq has `[2,3]`; `gsub("\\n$";"")` on `"line\n\n"` left the `\n`). A tie between two different matches is now decided by the exact engine (the `fancy-regex` route, which reads `$` as `(?=\n?\z)`), built only when it is needed, and the doubled newline is searched like a single one. A pattern with both `$` and `\z` is read by that engine alone. No change for a text that does not end in a newline. Left as they were, recorded in `limitations.md`: a pattern the exact engine refuses (a repeated empty group) and text that follows a `$` (`a$\n`).
