- **jq: `reduce`, `foreach`, `range`, `until` and `while` are no longer refused for running past 100,000 steps** (#5292). `reduce .users[] as $u (0; . + $u.score)` over a 699,050-element array stopped with `reduce: maximum iterations exceeded`, and `[range(200000)] | length` with `range: maximum iterations exceeded`, where jq answers `2443351017250` and `200000`. The step budget now counts fan-out instead of length: the first INIT fork's steps, and the first EXTRACT of each UPDATE's outputs, are free, so a fold with one INIT fork runs as long as its source does, while the forks past the first and the EXTRACT evaluations past each UPDATE's first are still charged (`reduce range(50001) as $x ((0,1,2); .+$x)` and a 1e10-evaluation `foreach range(100000) as $x (0; range(100000); empty)` still raise). A numeric `range` is built and handed over 100,000 values at a time, each batch before the next, so `reduce range(1000000) as $x (0; . + $x)` and `last(range(0; 2000000; 3))` answer as jq does, floats accumulate bit-identically, and `first(range(1e18))` and `limit` build only the batches they consume. A range now raises at 16,777,216 values (2^24) for a consumer that keeps asking, so `[range(1e18)]` and `range(nan)` do not grow until the process is killed, and `[range(20000000)]` is refused where jq answers; a range over non-numbers keeps the 100,000-value cap. `until` and `while` step in place without the 100,000-state cap: `0 | until(. >= 300000; . + 1)` and `[0 | while(. < 300000; . + 1)] | length` answer as jq does, and a loop that never ends (`until(false; . + 1)`, `[while(true; . + 1)]`) raises after 16,777,216 rounds where jq spins or is killed, while a round that forks keeps the 100,000-state cap that bounds the tree and the stack. The 10,000-node cap of `recurse(f)` (#5378) and the 40,000-call cap of a recursive `def` (#5379) remain.
