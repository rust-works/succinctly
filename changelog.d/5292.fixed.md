- **jq: `reduce`, `foreach` and `range` are no longer refused for running past 100,000 steps** (#5292). `reduce .users[] as $u (0; . + $u.score)` over a 699,050-element array stopped with `reduce: maximum iterations exceeded`, and `[range(200000)] | length` with `range: maximum iterations exceeded`, where jq answers `2443351017250` and `200000`. A fold's first INIT fork is no longer charged against the step budget, so a fold with one INIT fork (the common case) runs as long as its source does, and only the forks past the first are metered, which is the product the budget was written against (`reduce range(50001) as $x ((0,1,2); .+$x)` still raises). `range` is built and handed over 100,000 values at a time, each batch before the next, so `reduce range(1000000) as $x (0; . + $x)` and `last(range(0; 2000000; 3))` answer as jq does, floats accumulate bit-identically, and `first(range(1e18))` and `limit` still build one batch. A range now raises at 67,108,864 values (2^26) for a consumer that keeps asking, so `[range(1e18)]` and `range(nan)` do not grow until the process is killed. The 100,000-step caps of `until`/`while` and the 10,000-node cap of `recurse(f)` remain.
