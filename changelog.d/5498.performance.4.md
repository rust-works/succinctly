- **jq: `--stream` reads a file or stdin a chunk at a time, so it holds neither the input nor the events** (#5498, slice 6).
  The stream reader took the whole file (or stdin) first and read its events out of that buffer, so a plain `--stream` walk and `--stream -n` with `inputs` still peaked at the size of the input (110 MB on a 107 MB document) where jq's 4 KB reads peak at 2 MB.
  It now makes jq's refills (a line, or 4095 bytes) from a buffered reader per file and from stdin, with the same refills as before: an empty file, a BOM at the front of the first file that has bytes, and a token that continues in the next file read as they did.
  `--validate`, `-s` and `--stream-errors` without `-n` keep the whole read.
  Equivalence is pinned by a test that reads 19 streams through both sources, cut into reads of 1 to 100,000 bytes and buffers of 1 to 8,192, and by `tests/jq_cli/i5498_chunked.rs` over a 170 KB line, multi-byte characters across chunks, lines of exactly 4095 and 4096 bytes, a BOM and 20,000 short lines, over a file and over stdin.
