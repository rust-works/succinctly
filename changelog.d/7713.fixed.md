- **`succinctly jq` answers a tree recursion whose definition uses `try`, `label`, `reduce`, an object, `first`, a local def or another def** (#7713). After #7460 a call at a pipe's head or as an operand was run to its value first only when its definition's body was made of the shapes a tail loop runs ahead of its call, so `def count: if type == "array" then (.[0] | count) + (.[1] | count) else 1 end; count` over a binary tree 13 deep, and `def t($n): if $n == 0 then own else t($n - 1) + t($n - 1) end` with `own` defined as `try 1`, `label $l | 1`, `reduce ...`, `{a: 1}.a`, `first(1)`, `length` or `tostring`, were refused with `exceeded maximum recursion depth` at about 8,192 leaves where jq 1.7.1 and 1.8.2 answer. The question now also reads those shapes when they answer at most once, a pipe that ends in such a call, and a call with arguments to another def; an operator settles two operands that are such calls, and a tail loop's stage that calls into a module reaching a second one (its forwarding stub is a def declared in the body) runs in place again. Those programs also run faster (60-90% less time on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, 10-54% on an Apple M4 Pro, output identical); other programs move within noise, at most +1-2% for two pipe operands ending in a builtin (`(.score | tostring) + (.id | tostring)`, +0.95% instructions).
