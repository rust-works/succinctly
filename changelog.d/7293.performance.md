- **jq: `.` and `-c .` over an object of more than 786,432 keys print it from its text, 2.2-3.6x fewer instructions** (#7293).
  The canonical scan that lets a document be echoed (`-c`) or re-indented (pretty) gave up on an object wider than
  the key-hash table's ceiling after hashing 786,432 of its keys, and the walk that then printed it hashed and sorted
  every key again; since #7271 that made pretty `.` over the 100 MB `wide` object (7.1 M keys) about 3% slower
  than before. The scan now settles such an object's keys with the same sort, so one without a repeated key is
  printed from its text. On that object `jq .` retires 9,294 M instructions instead of 20,526 M and `jq -c .`
  6,824 M instead of 19,971 M (AMD Ryzen 9 7950X); on an Apple M4 Pro they take 360 ms instead of 788 ms and
  265 ms instead of 761 ms, in 186 MiB instead of 320 MiB and 293 MiB, file or stdin.
  An object that wide which the scan declines now pays the scan up to the reason before it is declined, where
  the scan used to stop at 786,432 keys: a repeated key, wherever it sits, is found only at the object's end
  (+8% instructions and +3-4% wall on the 100 MB object), any other non-canonical text past the first
  786,432 keys costs the scan up to it (+7% instructions, +5% wall for a non-canonical last member), +3% at
  1.6 M keys and about 1% just past the ceiling.
