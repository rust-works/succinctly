- **jq: `.` and `-c .` over an object of more than 786,432 keys print it from its text, 2.2-3.6x fewer instructions** (#7293).
  The canonical scan that lets a document be echoed (`-c`) or re-indented (pretty) gave up on an object wider than
  the key-hash table's ceiling after hashing 786,432 of its keys, and the walk that then printed it hashed and sorted
  every key again; since #7271 that made pretty `.` over the 100 MB `wide` object (7.1 M keys) about 3% slower
  than before. The scan now settles such an object's keys with the same sort, so one without a repeated key is
  printed from its text. On that object `jq .` retires 9,166 M instructions instead of 20,526 M and `jq -c .`
  6,824 M instead of 19,971 M (AMD Ryzen 9 7950X); on an Apple M4 Pro they take 360 ms instead of 784 ms and
  266 ms instead of 756 ms, in 186 MiB instead of 320 MiB and 293 MiB, file or stdin.
  An object that wide with a repeated key pays the whole scan before it is declined: 8% more time and
  instructions on the 100 MB object with its last key repeating its first, about 1% on one just past the ceiling.
  Indentation is also written as slices of fixed length (64 units, then one slice per set bit of the rest) where
  #7271 wrote one slice whose length was known only at run time, a `memcpy` call per line: pretty `.` over the
  10 MB `users`, `numbers`, `wide` and `comprehensive` inputs retires 0.5-1.8% fewer instructions (7950X).
