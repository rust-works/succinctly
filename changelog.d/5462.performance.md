- **yq: the stages after a union share one rest pipe across the context list, so they are linear in the pipe's length** (#5462).
  `(., 1) | . + 1 | . + 1 | ...` ran the stages after the union once per entry of the context list, each entry with a
  rest pipe of its own over a slice nothing owned, so every entry copied the whole rest and every stage in it copied
  its rest again: quadratic in the stages after the union. One rest pipe now drives the list, and a list of several
  entries gets a pipe to own the stages, which keeps its tails once (#5404) and hands the same ones to every entry. A
  single entry runs the stages once and is unchanged. 100,000 values, interleaved min of 5 CPU seconds, output
  identical: `[range(100000)] | .[] | (., 1) | . + 1 | ... x n` n=3 0.295 s -> 0.225 s, n=8 1.120 s -> 0.614 s,
  n=20 5.967 s -> 1.661 s on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X; n=3 0.208 s -> 0.147 s, n=8 0.820 s -> 0.430 s, n=20 3.712 s ->
  1.313 s on an Apple M4 Pro. The same work with the multiplicity from `(1, 2) as $k | . | ...` (the control with the same
  output count) reads 1.689 s and 1.149 s at n=20 and is within 5% of the baseline at every n on both machines, and a
  one-stage pipe after the union (n=1) is within 2%.
