- **`succinctly jq` indexes a JSON document of 4 GiB or more** (#7537). Since #7425 such a document was read into a tree without an index, and a program that needed the whole-document index (`map(length) | add`, `[.[] | f]`, `first(.[])`, `sort`, `to_entries`, `del(.[0])`, `.[0] = 1`, `[paths]`), and every document that was not plain JSON (a repeated key, `nan`, nesting past 128), was refused at exit 5 where jq 1.7.1 answers. The index's 32-bit fields are counts of interest bits and parentheses, not text positions (the one position it cached, the sequential-select hint, is now 64-bit), so it now holds a text of any length with at most `u32::MAX` of each, that is at most 2,147,483,647 values (keys included): only a document past that, about two bytes a value, is still read without an index. On a 4,296,011,781-byte array of 1 MiB strings (measured once on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, not an interleaved benchmark), `map(length) | add` prints `4295999485` as jq does, in 3.8 s at 4.9 GB peak RSS, and the programs that copy the array take 11.6-14.4 s at 9.0 GB. Index size on ordinary inputs is unchanged (the hint is packed into 12 bytes, which fit where the 8-byte one did), and every document shorter than 4 GiB is still indexed in place, without the length-past-4-GiB count: instructions rise 0.01-0.16% on record-by-record NDJSON and 0.00-0.28% on single documents over 1 and 10 MB inputs (cachegrind, Ryzen 9 7950X).
