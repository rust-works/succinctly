- **jq: `add` and `min`/`max` over a document array read each number's value without copying its spelling** (#7278). Both folds built every element as an owned number literal, with a heap copy of its text that they dropped unread. `add` computes a new number, and `min`/`max` print only the winner, which they take from the document. `add` now adds each number after the first as its value alone; the first still keeps its spelling, so `[1.0] | add` prints `1.0`. `min`/`max` key each number by its literal's double and integer and read two spellings off the document only when the doubles tie, as jq's exact literal comparison does. Over the `numbers` pattern, interleaved with an A/A control and output identical to jq 1.7.1's: `.numbers|add` on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X 12.1 -> 10.5 ms at 1 MB, 99 -> 83 ms at 10 MB, 960 -> 803 ms at 100 MB, and on an Apple M4 Pro 12.5 -> 11.1, 89 -> 78, 847 -> 736 ms; `.numbers|max` 10.5 -> 8.2, 84 -> 62, 815 -> 597 ms (7950X) and 11.8 -> 9.9, 84 -> 69, 791 -> 647 ms (M4 Pro); `[.numbers|min,max]` 1462 -> 1041 ms (7950X) and 1398 -> 1087 ms (M4 Pro) at 100 MB, now ahead of jq's 1581 and 1161 ms. Instructions at 10 MB fall 12-23% on the 7950X and 20-31% on the M4 Pro; `sort`, `[.[]|floor]|add` and identity are unchanged in instructions.
