- **jq: the regex builtins compile a pattern once, not once per input value** (#5531). `test`, `match`, `capture`, `sub`, `gsub`, `scan`, `split` and `splits` built their regex again for every value, and `regex::Regex::new` dominated a short-string match. A pattern seen a second time now has its compiled form kept, per thread, for the last 16 `(mode, pattern, flags)` (errors too, so a refused pattern is not translated and compiled again per input; `fancy-regex` patterns too; a pattern over 1 KiB is never kept). Over a 20,000-element array of short strings (user time, interleaved A/B, output identical): `test("[a-z]+[0-9]{2,}x?")` 860 ms to 7 ms on a Ryzen 9 7950X and 762 ms to 9 ms on an Apple M5 Max; a literal `test("item5")` 38 ms to 7 ms and 40 ms to 9 ms; `sub("[0-9]+";"#")` 414 ms to 13 ms and 380 ms to 14 ms. A pattern that is different for every input (7,919 distinct) costs 1.02x on both: it is only kept on its second sighting, because keeping every one cost 6% on the 7950X.
