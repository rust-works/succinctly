- **jq: `del(.)`, and `del(.[]?)` over a scalar or empty container, no longer cross the
  reindex bridge** (#4283). Both targets have the same paths whatever `.` is, so the owned
  evaluators (both of them: `eval_generic`'s and `eval.rs`'s eager and streaming routes) now
  answer them directly: `del(.)` is `null`, and `del(.[]?)` over a scalar, `[]` or `{}` is the
  input itself, not written out, indexed and decoded back (its number-literal spelling kept). The
  only allocation left per member is the member's own decode (`[.[] | del(.[]?)]` over 2,000
  integers: 6,022 -> 2,022 allocator calls; strings 10,022 -> 2,022; booleans and nulls
  2,022 -> 22). jq mode only: yq answers both differently and keeps its route.
  Interleaved A/B on an M4 Pro, min of 15, output identical: `[.[] | del(.)]` and
  `[.[] | del(.[]?)]` over 1M integers -47%, 300k strings -57% to -58%, 1M mixed scalars
  -47% to -48% (-52.6% instructions retired on the integer row); a container control
  (`[.[] | del(.a)]` over 200k objects) +0.4%, and `succinctly yq -R` `del(.)`/`del(.[]?)`
  over 300k lines within +0.02% instructions.
