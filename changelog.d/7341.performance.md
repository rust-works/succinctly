- **jq: `--seq` windows carry on past more of the stretches that are not plain records** (#7341).
  A `--seq` stream from one file of 4 MiB or more, or a pipe, used to hold the rest of the stream from a stretch
  holding a value jq reads and RFC 8259 does not (`nan`, `012`, nesting past 128 levels, invalid UTF-8 in a
  string), from one over 4 MiB or that drew over 1024 warnings, and from one that ran to the stream's end. Such
  values are now read where they are. A long stretch is read in 64 KiB pieces, each cut at an RS that starts a
  line, and one that runs to the end is handed on from its last such RS. A window ends once 65536 warnings wait
  for it. At 100 MiB a stream of `nan` records holds 14.7 MiB instead of 257.2 on an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X and 10.4
  instead of 290.8 on an Apple M4 Pro, and runs 54% and 43% shorter. One whose first record is `nan`, or draws
  3000 warnings, holds 11.8-12.3 MiB instead of 234 on the 7950X and 7.1-8.1 instead of 259 on the M4 Pro, 10-17%
  shorter. A 5 MB stream that warns once a byte holds 21-23 MiB instead of 171-200. Output unchanged.
