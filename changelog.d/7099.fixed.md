- **yq: a write and `-P` keep the first document's `%YAML` directive and print its leading comments as yq does** (#7099). The text yq sets aside in front of the first document and prints back before it lost parts on every route that builds the output from the document's comment tree (a write such as `.a = 3` or `del(.x)`, `sort_keys`, `to_entries`, `-P`). A `%YAML 1.2` line was dropped, with its `---` too when nothing else was in front of it, and `# a` / `%YAML 1.2` / `---` printed two blank lines in its place. A `%YAML` line after `---` was held as the first node's head comment, so a filter that moved that node (`to_entries`, `[.[1], .[0]]`, `{"x": .}`, `.[0] | ...`) carried it into the document, where neither yq nor `succinctly yq` can read it back. In a header with no `---`, a comment or `%YAML` line indented by blanks lost its indent (yq prints the comment as written and the `%YAML` line as `#   %YAML 1.2`). A comment after `---` over an empty `[]` or `{}` root was dropped, even by a write that fills the root. A line of only blanks after `---` printed twice when a comment followed it, and a tag or anchor on the root's line (`!!str 5`, `&x {}`) dropped the whole header. A head written to a scalar root (`. head_comment = "x"`, `|=` and `comments =` too) now removes the header as yq does, where it left the header in place. Checked against yq v4.53.3 by 6,864 generated documents (headers of comments, indented comments, blank lines, directives, markers and a marker with a comment after it, over 13 kinds of root, tagged and anchored ones among them, under 23 filters): 1,822 now match where they did not, none that matched stops, no output glues a marker onto the content, and the outputs that cannot be read back (where yq's own can be) fall from 54 to 19, all of which `main` prints too. Still different: a comment after `---` that `to_entries`, `with_entries` or a construction moves with the first node, a comment indented by blanks after `---` (both #7240), an operator that rebuilds the root (`map(.)`, `{"x": .} | .x`), a header with several `---` lines and something between them, and the `head_comment` read operator over a header.
