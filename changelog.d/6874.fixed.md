- **DSV: a CRLF line ending is the record terminator** (#6874). A file written with `\r\n` line endings (Excel, Windows tools, RFC 4180) left the `\r` in the last field of every record, and a last field that was quoted ended in `\r` and not in `"`, so its quotes stayed in the value: `a,b` / `1,"x y"` with CRLF read as `["a","b\r"]` and `["1","\"x y\"\r"]` through `succinctly jq --input-dsv`, `sjq`, and the library's `Dsv::rows`, `DsvRow::fields` and `DsvRow::get`, with no error. `DsvCursor::current_field`, which every accessor goes through, now leaves a `\r` out of a field that ends at a `\n` or at the end of the text; a `\r` in front of a delimiter or inside a quoted field is content as before, and the index is unchanged. Checked against Python's `csv` module on 6,400 random tables written with LF and CRLF terminators, comma and tab, both quoting modes and fields holding quotes, newlines, CRLF and non-ASCII text: the old build differed on 1,368 of 2,988 and the new one on none, and the same tables read the same under LF. A lone `\r` is still content, so a file with `\r`-only line endings is one record (recorded in `docs/parsing/dsv.md`).
