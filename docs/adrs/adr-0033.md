# ADR-0033: One Model of jq's Input Reader Entered at Sync Points (#7987)

## Status

🟡 Proposed (2026-11-27). Step 1 of #7987. Nothing here binds until the owner accepts it.
[ADR-0018](adr-0018.md) still decides every behaviour, and its #7963 amendment decides what the
reader owes jq: the first fault exactly (E1), a NUL outside a string as a parse error (E2), and jq
1.7.1 for every other reader fact, with three listed 1.8.2 exceptions. This record decides the
mechanism that keeps that promise. It does not change the promise.

The prototype is the branch `spike-reader-model-coherent` (base `0dac2e2d7`, pushed). The design
investigation behind it is not committed; its figures are quoted here with the build they were
measured on.

## Context

`succinctly jq` reads input through a semi-index, not through jq's parser
([semi-indexing.md](../architecture/semi-indexing.md)). At the edges of the input jq's answer is a
property of how its reader is built: `fgets` into a 4,096-byte buffer, one parser for the whole run
that is never told where a file ends, and "drop the rest of this read" on a fault. succinctly has
no such reader. It reproduces the consequences one at a time, per route.

About 27,000 lines of the jq runner do that (the design counts about 27,400, tests excluded):
`jq_runner.rs`, `jq_runner/window.rs`, `jq_seq_reader.rs`, `jq_stream_errors.rs`,
`src/jq/parse_message.rs` and smaller files. `jq_runner.rs` has more than sixty `static` and
`thread_local!` declarations, most of them carrying a decision from one input pass to the next.
Each route decides each edge fact again:

- the byte-order mark has 13 matchers (`leading_bom_len`, `stream_bom_cuts`, `bom_prefix`,
  `WindowedFile::skip_front_bom`, and nine more);
- the NUL byte has 5 rules, among them the rewrite `blank_nul_number_tails` at six call sites;
- jq's 4,095-byte read has 2 constants and 7 formulas, and only 3 of them count the mark's share of
  the first read;
- the fault's resume point has 7 deciders, and line and column have 9 counters.

Several of those deciders are pre-passes that rewrite the user's bytes before a reader sees them
(`blank_nul_number_tails`, `strip_leading_json_bom`, `stitch_json_file_seams`,
`carry_cut_document`). Each then needs bookkeeping, such as `Seam` and `LossySource`, to report
positions over the bytes as they were. Thirteen hand-offs between routes rebuild "where jq's reader
stands" from different subsets of that state.

Local fixes keep making bugs:

- #7897 was made by #7437 and #7749: the NUL-tail rewrite moved a digit across a file boundary.
- #7842 was made by #7569.
- Spike C combined local fixes for the open reader issues, #7904, #7922 and #7937 among them. It
  made two new bugs while it was written: a misplaced fault behind a BOM lead, and a phantom value
  from #7922's fix meeting #7937's. It could not finish #7937 without another decider. Its six
  fixes added 282 lines and 6 new deciders across 5 edge facts.

### The options considered

- **A. A full port that reads every byte** and certifies stretches for the semi-index. It is right
  by construction and too slow: the first prototype round measured +606% instructions on
  `.users|length` over three 161 KB documents, +81% on `.` and +109% on `-n '[inputs]|length'`.
  It also loses lazy validation (#3035). Rejected.
- **B. One shared edge layer** under today's routes: one token set, one read formula, one BOM
  matcher. It gives each fact one definition but leaves the reader's state to the routes, which is
  where the fix-made bugs came from. Its core, one profile and one read arithmetic, is kept as
  steps 2 and 4 of the migration.
- **C. Targeted fixes.** The cheapest next week and the dearest over a year (above). Kept for
  fixes that are independent of the model. Rejected as the way to close the layer.
- **D. One reader model, entered at sync points, with the semi-index running between them.**
  D-lite is its spike: the model with a coherent, whole-release profile, wired only into the plain
  routes that read their input whole, and reading to the end once entered. Chosen (below).
- **G. Vendoring jq's C reader.** Exact by construction (0 differences on every set tried), but
  its memory is unbounded: a single 100 MB array peaked at 2.82 GB, about 26x. It also costs about
  4.6x succinctly's per-byte instructions over an edge region, 20.7k lines of C, +178 KB of binary
  and MSVC patches. Rejected as the reader. Kept as the test oracle (#7970).
- **E. Narrowing the promise.** Accepted as the #7963 amendment of ADR-0018 (E1, E2 and the
  1.7.1 pin). It is a policy, not a mechanism: the reader still has to keep the narrower promise.

### What the amendment removed from the model's scope

The design was written before #7963, so it carries more than is now owed:

- **Post-fault resumption.** jq resumes at its next read. After the first fault, E1 asks only for
  the floor: resume at the next line (or the next record separator under `--seq`), print only whole
  values that lie after the fault, and print no value neither release prints. Later faults,
  positions after the first fault and U+FFFD counts after it are best effort.
- **NUL truncation.** Neither 1.7.1's `strlen` cut of a newline-less read nor 1.8.2's read-through
  is reproduced. So the model emits no "NUL-ended number" as a C-string prefix, and the profile has
  no NUL switch.
- **A mixture of releases.** There is one shipped profile, not a per-route choice and not a
  per-fact one. The spike's default of 1.8.2 and its 4,091-byte reads with UTF-8 completion are not
  shipped. The depth limit is 1.7.1's 256, so the spike's depth exception, which gave
  neither-release answers (`i7470`, `i7611`), is gone.

## Decision

We will read jq-mode input through **one model of jq 1.7.1's reader**. It is entered only at sync
points, and the semi-index runs between them.

### The model

The model is a Rust port of two pieces of jq:

- `jq_util_input_read_more` (`src/util.c`): the `fgets` reads, `current_line`, the file switch and
  `is_last`;
- the non-streaming path of `jv_parse.c`: the BOM matcher in `jv_parser_set_buf`, `scan`, the token
  function, `check_literal`, `found_string`, the fault path and the end-of-input checks.

It builds no values. It emits each value as the JSON text jq accepted, and each fault in jq's
words, each with the file and line jq would report. The crate's own value reader decodes the text.
On the prototype this is `Reader` (`read_more`, `next_input`, `next_item`, `position`) and `Parser`
in `src/bin/succinctly/jq_runner/reader_model.rs`.

### The profile

`ReaderProfile` holds every release-dependent switch of the reader. Each field names the jq source
it models. A profile is one whole release, never a switch-by-switch mixture: the prototype's first
profile paired 1.7.1's read size with 1.8.2's NUL reading, and gave answers neither release gives
on 7 inputs where both agree.

The shipped profile is jq 1.7.1, less the three exceptions the amendment lists:

| Exception                                                  | Issues       |
|------------------------------------------------------------|--------------|
| `--seq` reads every record                                 | #7718        |
| `--stream --slurp` keeps 1.8.2's order and its fault       | #7523, #7807 |
| a character straddling the 4,095-byte `-R` read is kept    | #5476        |

Each exception is a named entry with its reason, as the prototype's `EXCEPTIONS` list is, and a
test holds the shipped profile to the release plus that list. Every route reads its reader
constants from the profile, and from nothing else.

### Sync points and state

A **sync point** is a place where jq's parser is between top-level values at the start of one of
its reads: no token, no container stack and no pending value. There, jq's whole reader state is a
few numbers: the current file, its `current_line`, the parser's `line` and `column`, and the BOM
matcher's progress. Under `--seq` the parser's waiting-for-RS state and its whitespace flag are
added. Spike G checked that this is enough in jq's own C parser (its 1.8.2 build): it recorded
17.4 million sync points over 260 runs and resumed at 1,548 of them, all exact.

- **`ReaderState`** is that struct. It is the only thing a route may carry across a window, a file
  or a hand-off. It replaces the loose fields the hand-offs carry today, among them `Seam`'s read
  anchor, the `jq_seq_reader` seeds, `HANDED_ON_NO_RS_END`, `STREAM_BOM_LEADS` and
  `BROKEN_MARK_STREAM`.
- **`step(bytes)`** is the faithful port. It advances `ReaderState` over bytes the fast path did
  not certify, runs until the next sync point, and hands back.
- **`skip_plain(summary)`** is the closed form. It advances `ReaderState` over a stretch the fast
  path has already read and found plain (complete values and white space only), from facts the
  route already holds: lengths, newline counts and where each file starts. It reads no byte.
- **The `skip_plain == step` property.** For every plain stretch, `skip_plain` gives the same state
  as `step`, compared on value, message, line, column and resume point. A property test over
  generated plain stretches pins it. This is the lessons log's rule ("one definition, plus a test
  that the call sites agree", [lessons.md](../optimizations/lessons.md)) applied to the reader.

### Triggers

A route runs the semi-index as today and enters `step` from the last sync point only when one of
these holds. Each is a fact the route already computes:

- **a byte-order mark that is not whole**: the stream's first byte is `EF` and its first three
  bytes are not a whole mark in one source;
- **a fault**: the fast path refused something it was asked to decode. Under E2 a NUL outside a
  string is a fault, and the UTF-8 pass that every route runs already finds NULs
  (`scan_utf8_and_nul`). A NUL is a trigger only in this sense;
- **invalid UTF-8**: the route substituted bytes (`scan.utf8` on the default route and the lazy
  front, `LossySource` on the materializing route). #7938 showed this is needed: it has invalid
  UTF-8 and no NUL, mark or fault, so the model was never entered.

**A file seam is not a trigger.** jq's parser does not see the boundary, and a value that
straddles two files is not an edge. With a seam as a trigger the perf guard's four `split*` rows (a
well-formed document cut across 2 or 16 files) failed at +15% to +375%.

At the end of input, `skip_plain` answers the position without stepping.

### Routes

The model is wired route by route, each in its own step:

- the **materializing** route of `read_inputs` (step 5, the prototype's wiring);
- the **default** route of `run_jq_on_stack_floor`, which hands back to the semi-index at the next
  sync point instead of declining to the materializing route (step 6);
- the **lazy and windowed** routes, which carry `ReaderState` across windows and files and can
  resume windows after a fault (step 7);
- **`--seq`**, as the model's waiting-for-RS mode (step 8);
- **`--stream`**, with jq's `stream_token` as the model's second token function (step 9).

`-R` uses no parser. It keeps its own line reader and reads its read size and its straddle
exception from the profile.

When every route carries the model, the rewriting pre-passes and their bookkeeping are deleted:
`blank_nul_number_tails` and its call sites, `strip_leading_json_bom`'s partial cases,
`stitch_json_file_seams`, `carry_cut_document`'s rewrite, `Seam`'s shares and leads, and every
thread-local that carried reader state (step 10).

### Open questions for the owner

1. **#5804's wording.** The model says `Malformed BOM` on the first read, as both releases do.
   `limitations.md` records `Invalid numeric literal` there for the plain routes (#5804), and
   `i7851` and `i7863` pin it. The amendment declines free wording (E6) and says the #5804 wording
   stays. Should step 5 retire #5804 as a move towards jq, or keep it?
2. **The NaN payload.** The prototype's 1.7.1 profile has a fourth exception, `nan_payload`: the
   crate's one number grammar refuses `NaN5` as jq 1.8 does (#7522, recorded on the compliance
   page). The amendment lists three exceptions. Is #7522 a reader fact to list, or a number-grammar
   divergence that stays outside the profile?
3. **Validation inside an edge region.** Between sync points validation stays lazy (#3035, the
   #2103 amendment). Inside an edge region the model validates what it steps over, as jq does, so
   a malformed neighbour of a fault is diagnosed where the lazy path alone would not have read it.
   The design asks to accept that, as #7750 accepts it for a file cut.
4. **Where the model lives.** The prototype puts it in the CLI. E2 sends `fromjson`, `--argjson`
   and `--slurpfile` through the same rule. Should they share the model, which would move it into
   `src/jq/`?

## Consequences

### Measured on the spike

D-lite, against jq 1.7.1 and 1.8.2 (spike report; most runs under its default `jq182` profile):

| What                                    | Result                                                                    |
|-----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Issues open when the spike ran          | fixed #7922, #7937, #7947, #7948 (both shapes), #7921's residual          |
| 30,000-case corpus (seed 1)             | 86 of `main`'s 198 neither-release rows fixed, 0 new, under each profile  |
| Inputs where the releases agree (46)    | all match, under both profiles                                            |
| Full `jq_cli_tests` (`jq182`, x86_64)   | 5,219 pass, 27 fail (debug build)                                         |
| Input-filtered tests (`jq171`, ARM64)   | 850 pass, 7 fail                                                          |

- Under `jq182`, 20 of the 27 failures pin a 1.7.1 answer where the spike gave 1.8.2's, 2 pin
  1.7.1 where it gave neither (`i7470`, `i7611`, the depth exception), and 5 pin an answer neither
  release gives (`i5654`, `i7851`, `i7863`, one `i7921` test and `i7934_nul`). Under the 1.7.1
  pin the first 22 should pass, but the full suite was not run under `jq171`.
- Under `jq171` the 7 failures are those 5 neither-release pins, `i7807` and `i7024_files`.
  `i7807` pins 1.8.2's slurp order under `-n -s`, with and without `--stream`; the listed
  exception names `--stream --slurp`, so whether it covers the plain row is for #7971.
  `i7024_files` is the macOS `/dev/fd/N` bug below, fixed on the branch and not re-run. Of the 5
  neither-release pins, the NUL ones move with #7966 and the BOM ones with question 1.
- Under `jq171`, 112 of the 742 corpus rows that equal only 1.8.2 on `main` become 1.7.1. That is
  the direction #7971 asks for.
- The model is not wired into the windowed routes, so #7904's shapes and #7938 at 4 MiB were not
  fixed by it.

Instruction counts were taken on thin builds, not the shipped one (terminus, x86_64; `main` A/A
0.13% on startup, 0.012% or less elsewhere). Under `jq171` every row was flat apart from startup at
+0.04%. Under `jq182` the `-n inputs` rows rose 1.36% to 3.88%, but the prototype head was already
+2.92% in the same build, on a route the model never runs, so the rise is probably a build
artefact. The earlier prototype (at `f884a5148`, wired into the same routes) measured at most
+0.016% per record and +0.04% on startup on the shipped fat-LTO build, with the perf guard 48 of
48 within threshold (AMD Ryzen 9 7950X).

### Positive

- An edge fact has one decider, with its state included. A new route cannot disagree with an old
  one about a mark, a seam, a read boundary or a fault, because it has only a `ReaderState` to
  decide with.
- The pre-passes that rewrite the user's bytes go, with their bookkeeping and thread-locals.
- The reader's release is written down in one place. A move to jq 1.9 is one profile change.
- The windowed routes can come back to windows after a fault, which #7260 records as impossible
  today.

### Negative

- **Two readers coexist until step 10.** The model and the deciders it replaces both ship, behind
  a switch (`SUCCINCTLY_READER_MODEL=0` on the prototype). This is the main risk. A route that
  hands off without `ReaderState` is wrong whichever reader is on the other side: the prototype's
  round 2 lost a fault that way (`i7024_many_files`).
- **Everything rests on the port being faithful.** The oracle is the check, not review alone.
- **Inputs with edges get slower** over the stretch they spend in the model: the earlier port cost
  about 170 instructions per byte where the lazy cursor spends about 27 on a field lookup. With
  hand-back that stretch is a read; the spike, without hand-back, reads the rest of the input.
- **Known gaps from the spike:**
  - the invalid-UTF-8 trigger takes a large substituted stream off the lazy route, which costs
    memory (#5661, #5853; two lib tests fail on it);
  - a line base seeded past 2³² is ignored (#7472; two lib tests);
  - macOS `/dev/fd/N` reopens at the current offset, which broke the fault path's second read.
    Fixed on the spike branch only.
- **Tests that pin today's mixture change.** Each change is the profile or E1/E2 showing through,
  and is recorded in `limitations.md`, not made silently.

### Gates

Every step is gated by the in-process oracle,
[`jq-reader-oracle`](../../jq-reader-oracle/README.md) (#7970). PR #7992 (#7986, open) wires a
fixed subset of it into CI as the `reader-oracle` sweep.

- **Oracle:** `jq-reader-oracle diff` on seeds 1 to 3 shows `BUG(agree)` = 0, and `split=1.8.2`
  holds only the listed exceptions. `main` is not there yet: the #7992 sweep lists residuals
  (#7989, #7991), and a step must add no new signature.
- **Performance:** the perf guard stays within threshold, and the reader rows are A/B'd on the
  shipped build profile (one codegen unit, fat LTO), against the design's bar of +0.1% instructions
  per record and +0.3% on startup. The spike's thin-build counts are not usable for this (#7987).
  The A/B follows [benchmarking.md](../guides/benchmarking.md)'s method.
- **Tests:** the input-related `jq_cli_tests` pass, re-pinned to the 1.7.1 profile where #7963
  says so.

## Migration plan

Each step is one PR with `jq-reader-oracle diff` as its gate. Steps 2 to 4 change no behaviour and
can start now. Steps 5 onwards follow #7966 (E2) and #7967 (E1), so the model is never asked to
reproduce behaviour the amendment dropped.

1. **Push the prototype and propose this ADR.**
2. **`ReaderProfile`** with the 1.7.1 pin and the three listed exceptions, read by every route's
   constants. No behaviour change.
3. **The model, unused:** `ReaderState`, `step`, `skip_plain` and the `skip_plain == step`
   property test. Nothing in `src/` calls it yet.
4. **One read arithmetic.** The routes' read formulas (`EofTail`'s, `LineCounter`'s and their
   siblings) call `skip_plain`.
5. **The materializing route** enters the model on its triggers (a fault, which includes E2's NUL;
   a mark that is not whole; invalid UTF-8).
6. **The default route** hands back at the next sync point.
7. **The lazy and windowed routes** carry `ReaderState`. The spike estimates the resume at 150 to
   250 lines. This should cover #7980's route, to be confirmed.
8. **`--seq`.**
9. **`--stream`.** The sync point of the streaming parser is this step's to define: spike G's check
   covered the non-streaming parser only.
10. **Delete** the rewriting pre-passes, then shrink `limitations.md`'s reader sections to the
    profile.
